## 2. The four suspect scenes: rewrites

### 2.1 Diagnosis (why they read as lecture) [V/E]
- After the turn the narrator speaks 94% of the words and only two characters speak a word. Each beat is "name, claim, chip".
- Each scholar is introduced with a job title and a verb of opinion ("notes", "suggested", "call it"). That is 7.5 proper nouns per 100 words in the suspects against UF's 4.7 [V, teardown].
- Pictures illustrate the sentence (circle on a skull, block stack) rather than something happening.

### 2.2 The devices (all inside the integrity rules: no invented words for named scholars)
1. **The sailor as audience proxy.** He is already made up (beat 07), so his lines can be ours. He asks the viewer's question; the narrator answers; the chip carries the source. Max 8 words each. He reappears in Suspect 1 only.
2. **Polyphemus and the smiths as performers in Suspect 2.** Gag lines for Polyphemus outside Homer ("I keep sheep.") are clearly gag in context (a forge, a lamb under his arm). Tag them (gag) and keep them to one or two.
3. **Evidence as objects.** An empty drawer for "ancient sources", a stack of blocks with one pulled out, a mule pair that cannot move a block, a replay of Act 1's own pictures as the folktale.
4. **Verbatim chips as a duel.** Use only text we hold verbatim: Mayor (C7, Discover), Colavito's note (C4 "a modern myth invented, apparently, by Abel himself", read directly [V]), Witton and Hing's "becomes baseless" (C4, but only via Wikipedia's quotation: mark "AS QUOTED"). Never paraphrase inside quotation marks, and put 3-6 words on screen at a time, not the sentence.
5. **A mini-twist and a button per suspect:** S1 both famous scenes trace to Abel; S2 Homer's Cyclopes sneer at Zeus, Hesiod's work for him; S3 the giants are not the cause, they are the explanation; S4 you have already watched this story.
