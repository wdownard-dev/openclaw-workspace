## 2. The checks you asked for, in short

1. **(ours) sentences (about 41):** 3 FIX on an invented fact (047 sword, 079 laugh, 090 no one speaks); the rest are PASS or PASS (colour). Two (063, 084) are stretched but defensible.
2. **Rams details:** **V5-056 (stone rolled away in the morning): verified**; Butler adds that he "at once put it back again", so the shot must show it closed again. **V5-126 (men drive the flock down to the ship): verified**, with the caveat that only the male sheep (the ones they hung under) went out, so "whole flock" is loose. V5-110 (best ram, clings to its belly) verified.
3. **"Eats two men at a time" (005):** PASS. Three meals of two; Butler writes "two of my men at once" for the first.
4. **Closing-chapter hedges:**
   - Abel 1914 as "imagined sailors... a modern claim, not ancient evidence": PASS. Source is Abel via Colavito; Boccaccio's 14th-century giant-bone link is about giant bones, not elephants.
   - Hesiod "around seven hundred B.C.": PASS. But "one eye" is Hesiod, and "at a forge" is not (160, FIX minor).
   - Tiryns "twelve fifty": **FIX (hedge)**; 1250 is the Lion Gate (Mycenae), not Tiryns.
   - Hackman 221: PASS. Iceland, Portugal (border with Galicia), Russia, Nights: all in the OCR.
   - "Many scholars think the tale is older than Homer": PASS as hedged; secondary only.
5. **Butler quotes:** 145 is exact. 119-120 matches, with the final sentence cut mid-clause. 152-153 matches the words with an added comma. 080's "keep Noman for the last" is a trace note, not spoken. 098 is a paraphrase of Butler's split wording; the Greek is unverified.
