# TOOLING-EVAL.md: is Remotion the right tool for the UF-style pipeline? (2026-11-12)

Author: Tooling subagent. Nothing generated, uploaded or sent; no images, no TTS. Write scope: `projects/bluepill/video2/`.
William's question: "If Remotion is not the best tool for the job, I want to know what is and use that."
Labels: **[V]** verified today (fetched, run or measured on this machine), **[E]** my estimate or inference, **[U]** could not verify.

## 0. Verdict

**Stay with Remotion. The problem with the draft was its design, not the tool.**

1. I built the same 20 s UF-style sequence twice: in Remotion and in the highest-ranked alternative (a small scripted renderer: Node + sharp composites only the frames that change and pipes them to ffmpeg). The stills are indistinguishable (`video2/spike/out/compare_stills.jpg`). The alternative renders about 2x faster when the design is mostly held frames (the UF recipe) and about 2x slower when every frame moves. Neither speed gap changes what we can make. [V]
2. The draft's 21-minute render came from its design (72% of frames in motion, heavy cards and maps), not from Remotion being slow: the same Remotion renders a UF-style hold-and-pop sequence at 0.59x realtime (120 s in 70 s) against the draft's 2.09x (612 s in 1,279 s). [V for both numbers, E for the attribution]
3. No credible candidate removes a blocker Remotion has. The paid tools I checked are worse for us: DaVinci Resolve Free lost all scripting in v21.1 (Sept 2026), Resolve Studio and Adobe need a GUI app and, for Resolve, a GPU we do not have; Adobe has no Linux build; CapCut has no official API; Descript's API edits transcripts, not sprite animation; Opus Agent generates its own visuals. [V where cited, E where marked]
4. Remotion's real gap was **audio**: its bundled ffmpeg lacks `ebur128`, compressor and limiter, so the draft used hand-written Node DSP. The ffmpeg 7.0.2 build at `/home/williamdownard/openclaw-sites/bin/ffmpeg` has `sidechaincompress`, `amix`, `loudnorm`, `ebur128`, `alimiter`, `rubberband`. A 2-voice mix with a ducked bed, loudnorm and limiter ran in 0.54 s for 20 s of audio and measured -15.5 LUFS, -1.0 dBTP. [V] **Fix: Remotion renders the picture, full ffmpeg mixes and muxes.**
5. **Cost to William: $0.** Remotion is free for individuals and companies of up to 3 people, all features, unlimited commercial use. [V, remotion.dev/docs/license/pricing, fetched today]

What to change inside the Remotion project is in section 5. A fallback (the spike renderer) is kept in `video2/spike/sharpff/` if render time ever becomes the bottleneck.

## 3. The spike (same 20 s sequence, two tools)

**Sequence** (`video2/spike/common/storyboard.json`; first 20 s of `video1/audio/words.json`): 6 hard-cut shots. A map with an animated route and a "SICILY" label popping on word 10; a white cutaway where the dwarf elephant pops on "stood", a "UNDER 1 m" label on "metre" and Odysseus on "tall"; a painted plate (NB01) held static with an "ONE HOLE" label popping on "hole"; a white counter card (year ticking 1900 to 1914 from "1914", 15 steps/s) with a label; two more painted plates (NB05 with "CYCLOPS" popping on the word, NB10 held). Assets reused: `video1/images/NB01/NB05/NB10/NP01`, `tests/chars_v3/cut/odysseus_A.png`, Natural Earth geometry from `pipeline/remotion-v1/src/data/med.json`. No images generated. Every event is anchored to a **word index**, so a script or timing change is one command: `node resolve.js words.json timeline.json`.

Both renderers read the same `timeline.json` and the same `anim.js` (easeOutBack pop of 0.2 s with 1.4 overshoot, 15 fps stop-motion steps for the route and the counter). Run both with the commands in `video2/spike/README.md`.

**Visual result** (`video2/spike/out/compare_stills.jpg`, 8 timestamps, top two rows Remotion, bottom two state renderer): the two are visually the same. Same font (Fira Sans Condensed ExtraBold), same pop, same map and route. **What the spike says**
- For the UF recipe (91% frozen frames [V, Editor]), compositing only the changed frames halves the render time. For a 10-minute episode I extrapolate 5-6 min (Remotion) against 3 min (state renderer) at 1080p, and about 23 min against 16 min at 4K. [E: linear extrapolation from 120 s, with real episodes heavier than this sequence]
- The state renderer is only fast while frames hold. One slow push-in on every painted shot makes it 2x slower than Remotion (Chrome's compositor does transforms cheaply). Our design rule "hold still" is what makes it fast; our draft's design rule "push in on everything" is what made Remotion slow.
- Neither tool is hard for an agent. Both took minutes to write; what took time in the draft was designing shots, not the engine.
- **Not tested** in the spike: word-synced captions (draft already has them in Remotion), speech bubbles, many pose swaps, SFX sync, long-run memory, and Remotion's heavier components (the draft's maps and diagrams). The draft's 14.4 frames per second render is the only number I have for those; the attribution to design is [E].

## 4. Ranking and reasons

1. **Remotion + full ffmpeg for audio.** Already built, 72.8k-star project pushed today, free for us, renders the UF recipe at 0.59x realtime, retimes with no code edits, strongest text layout and component model, and an agent can inspect it with stills. Score 33 once audio moves to ffmpeg.
2. **Scripted state renderer (sharp + ffmpeg).** Equal quality, about 2x faster on held frames, but we would own an engine with no preview player and DIY text. Best fallback or fast draft renderer; not worth switching now.
3. **Revideo.** Fine engine, same render class as Remotion, smaller ecosystem, owned by a company with its own product. No requirement it meets that Remotion misses.
4. **Pure ffmpeg filter graph.** Excellent for audio and final assembly (use it there), awkward for animated routes and text.
5. **Motion Canvas.** Great for explainer animation, editor-first workflow is the wrong shape for agent-run production.
6. **MLT / Kdenlive / Shotcut.** Possible, but we would learn an obscure XML toolchain to get less than ffmpeg already gives.
7. **Blender VSE.** Heavy install, weak text and ducking, not what it is for.
8. **Editly.** Stale; clips and transitions, not sprites.
9. **MoviePy.** Python convenience, slow for this.
10. **OpenShot, Rive/Lottie.** No fit for agent-run production here.
Not usable: DaVinci Resolve (free: no scripting; Studio: GUI plus GPU), Adobe, CapCut, Descript (wrong job), Opus Agent (own visuals).

## 5. What to change in the Remotion project (instead of a migration)

This is the whole plan. It takes about 1.5 days of agent work [E] and costs nothing.

1. **Storyboard compiler.** Copy the spike's idea into `pipeline/remotion-v3/` (new project, leaves v1 alone): a `storyboard.json` per episode (schema in FORMAT-BIBLE section 11) anchored to word indices, `resolve.js` turns it into absolute seconds from the ElevenLabs word timestamps, Remotion reads the resolved JSON as input props. A script or voice change is one command, as measured above.
2. **Component library for the recipe**: `Plate` (static painted image, no camera), `Cutaway` (white background plus sprites), `Pop` (sprite or label with the shared easing), `Counter` (stop-motion steps), `RouteMap` (route draws in 15 fps steps), `Stamp`, `SourceChip`, `Bubble`. No Ken Burns, no crossfade component exists, so the draft's design cannot be rebuilt by accident.
3. **Audio outside Remotion.** Render the picture silent. Mix voices, music, SFX and room tone with the full ffmpeg (`sidechaincompress`, `amix`, `loudnorm`, `alimiter`, verified), then mux. Delete the Node DSP.
4. **Render ladder.** Agent QA on stills and `--frames` ranges; animatic at `--scale=0.5` (30 s for 120 s of video measured); final 1080p at `--concurrency=11`. Keep the 4K path (45.6 s per 20 s measured) for when 3840 px plates exist; today's 1536 px plates would only be upscaled.
5. **Fallback trigger.** If a 10-minute 1080p render exceeds 20 minutes under the new design, port the hold-heavy shots to the spike's state renderer (it already reads the same timeline) or lower image quality. If the team ever exceeds 3 people, Remotion needs a company licence: $25 per month per Creators seat, or Automators at $0.01 per render with a $100 monthly minimum [V pricing page].
