## How to read this file
- Each beat is `**NN · SPEAKER** spoken line`, then a `visual` note and a `trace` note. Speakers: NARR narrator (George), ODY Odysseus, POLY Polyphemus, CREW the crew (also the sailor in suspect 1), CYC2 the other Cyclopes (the neighbours in the cave, the smiths in suspect 2). TEXT-CHIP lines are on screen only and are never spoken.
- Visual grammar (from the reference and `FORMAT-BIBLE.md`): hard cuts and pop-ins only; a new picture or pop roughly every sentence or two; no camera moves of any kind (no pan, no push, no shake, no tilt), no wipes, no crossfades; pose changes are swaps; source chips ride under the line and are 2.5 s long; no burned-in captions on story shots; no full-sentence quote cards.
- Trace: research id plus label. **V** verified in a text I (or the fact-checker) read; **E** inference, or reached through a secondary source; **U** not verified, not used. "(gag)" means the line is our joke and carries no factual claim. "(dramatised)" means invented or tidied dialogue, covered by the disclosure at beat 04. "(ours)" is an audience question or reaction with no factual content. **FC NN** = the fact-checker's row for V2 beat NN in `FACTCHECK-V2.md` (its Greek checks are in Appendix A); a bare "FC" with a note means that row passed or was fixed as stated.
- **Details verified for V2** (Butler, https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1727, Book 9): **N1** Odysseus and four men chosen by lot carry the stake; he hides it under dung. **N2** Polyphemus speaks to the ram: "My good ram... you lag last of all. Is it because you know your master has lost his eye?" **N3** Departure: first jeer, a mountaintop thrown just in front of the ship, the crew beg ("he has thrown one rock at us already"), the second shout with the name; then Telemus's prophecy ("a little insignificant weakling"), the offer of presents, and Odysseus answering that it will take more than Neptune to cure that eye. **N4** The prayer includes "late and in sore plight after losing all his men". **N5** Maron's twelve jars are "unblended"; Maron mixed twenty parts water to one of wine. **N6** Polyphemus's first words ("Strangers, who are you?... traders, or... rovers?"); Odysseus asks for hospitality in Zeus's name; Polyphemus answers that Cyclopes do not care about Zeus.
- **Details re-verified for V3** (this session, web_fetch): **N7** Abel 1914 as translated by Colavito (https://www.jasoncolavito.com/fossil-origin-of-the-cyclops.html): of the sailors, "probably the first", "perhaps in a beach cave", "In lighting a fire, they saw an elephant skull projecting from the clay"; of Empedocles, "reported such discoveries and believed them to be remnants of an extinct giant race" with no hedge; sockets "deep down on the sides"; Colavito's note: "this claim is a modern myth invented, apparently, by Abel himself." **N8** Hackman, *Die Polyphemsage in der Volksueberlieferung* (1904), archive.org OCR (https://archive.org/download/diepolyphemsage00hackgoog/diepolyphemsage00hackgoog_djvu.txt): the catalogue runs to "N:r 221"; it has an Icelandic entry (N:r 36), a Portuguese entry "from the border of Portugal and Galicia", Russian entries ("Grossrussisch"), and an Arabic entry, the Thousand and One Nights, Sindbad's third voyage. No hits for Korea or Ireland (so they are cut). **F1** Aeolus's bag (Odyssey 10.19-24) and Circe's wand (10.238, 319), in the Greek, per FACTCHECK-V2 beat 109. **N9** Discover Magazine page (https://www.discovermagazine.com/one-eyed-looking-mammoth-fossils-may-have-inspired-origins-of-the-cyclops-45824), full text: Mayor's quote is verbatim; the sentence about Alexander the Great and live elephants is the journalist's own prose, not Mayor's; Mayor also says "I think there's no doubt that fossils influenced the way the ancient Greeks and Romans pictured mythological stories about giants, monsters, and fabulous creatures."
