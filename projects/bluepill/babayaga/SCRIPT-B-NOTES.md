## Cut or changed from the SPINE (and why)
1. **Beat 14 (stump grave-houses) is cut.** No pre-1930 source found. Ralston (1873, p.138) describes the hut on fowl's legs that turns on a spoken formula and says nothing of grave-houses. The stump-grave reading is Propp (1946), post-1930, and it is also Slavique's 10:42. Cut keeps us clear of their order.
2. **Servants tale is Afanasyev no.103** (Voronezh, "Baba-yaga", second half of the ru.wikisource page "102-103"), in English as Ralston, *Russian Folk-Tales*, "The Baba Yaga", pp.139-142 (Ralston cites it as Afanasief i. no.3b). It matches the SPINE's four gifts exactly, so the geese-swans fallback is unused. Differences from the SPINE, which was written from memory:
   - the girl is sent for a needle and thread (not fire);
   - Baba Yaga does NOT send the gate, dog and birch after the girl and have them refuse. The girl simply passes them; AFTER she is gone, Baba Yaga beats each, and each answers with what she never gave it. B-105 to B-108 follow the text;
   - the "dogs" are plural, and Ralston says "doors" where Afanasyev says ворота (gate). Script says gate;
   - the SPINE's "servants" are the cat, the dogs, the doors, the birch and one maid.
   I cut the pursuit (river from the towel, forest from the comb, the oxen drinking the river) and the father shooting the stepmother, to keep the chapter to about 225 words. The comb and towel appear as props only (B-102).
3. The chores are compressed to one list (B-043) and one poppy-seed line (B-057). The hut-turning rhyme ("Little House, little House", W p.41) is cut.
4. The SPINE's Vasilisa "is eight". She is eight only when her mother dies; the hook now says "a young girl" (William 11/18).
5. The SPINE's rule 3 ("carry what was given in love") was replaced; the three rules are now (1) work pays for fire, (2) ask only about what you saw outside, (3) be kind to her staff, because each is a thing the texts show her enforcing. The blessing line stays in the tale (B-072, B-073) and is not turned into a rule.
