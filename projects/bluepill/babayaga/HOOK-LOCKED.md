# Cold open (locked by William, 2026-11-18; "young" replaces "eight-year-old" the same day)

One of the best-known Baba Yaga stories ends with a family burned to ashes. It starts when a stepmother sends a young girl into the forest to borrow fire. What the girl brings back is a skull with burning eyes, and it is exactly what she was sent for.

William's note: he did not know this story and it still made him want to watch. He ranks this and the Everglades hook as our two best. The shape is in VOICE.md section 2 (HOOK FORMULA).

Why "young": the first lock said "eight-year-old", but in Afanasyev, Ralston and Wheeler Vasilisa is eight only when her mother dies; the errand comes years later, when she is of marrying age. "Young" is true at the errand. William chose it over a named Vasilisa.

## Consequences for the spine
- The hook states the ending (a family burned to ashes) and the object (skull, burning eyes). The viewer's open question is HOW and WHY, not WHAT. Beat 10 therefore pays the mechanism: the skull's eyes follow the stepmother and stepsisters, and the lamp does exactly the job the errand named. Do not re-reveal the skull as a surprise in beat 10.
- The title is now "Baba Yaga Keeps Her Word: True Story of the Slavic Witch". "Never Touched Her" is dead (William 11/18): Baba Yaga never touching Vasilisa is true only in Wheeler; in Afanasyev and Ralston she drags the girl out and pushes her through the gate. Do not use the claim anywhere.
- Beat 1 (candle snuffed, "someone must go for fire") becomes the re-hook at 0:30-1:00, as a story beat.

## Fact-check list (research notes, not narration-facing)
1. "most famous Baba Yaga story" = Vasilisa the Beautiful (Afanasyev; Wheeler 1912). Verify it is the best-known.
2. "a family burned to ashes": stepmother and both stepsisters reduced to ashes by morning (W "ashes"; A "coal"; R "cinders").
3. "young girl": Vasilisa is of marrying age at the errand (A "стала невестой"; W p.35).
4. "sends ... to borrow fire": the errand for a light; the sender is the stepmother (stepsisters snuff the candle, per spine note 1).
5. "skull with burning eyes": the skull on a pole with eyes that blaze, taken from the fence by Baba Yaga and given to Vasilisa.
6. "exactly what she was sent for": Baba Yaga's line, "That is what they sent thee here for" (W p.52, R p.157, A).
