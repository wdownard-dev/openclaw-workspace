# HOOKS-R2.md: Baba Yaga, second hook round (2026-11-18)

Why round 1 felt weak: H2 and H3 explain the twist instead of showing it. H2 is a summary ("gives a visitor what the visitor came for"), H3 is a rule with no picture. William's own pick last time was a picture ("The sky is a skull"), so these lead with a picture and leave the next step open. Voice per VOICE.md: no em dashes, at most one question, hook answers nothing. About 40 spoken words each.

**R1. The skull at dawn** (picture plus ending glimpse) RECOMMENDED
"At dawn, a skull on a pole stops in front of a woman. Its eyes are burning, and they follow her around the room. By the time the sun is fully up, there is a small heap of ash where the stepmother used to be."
[Afanasyev/Wheeler, beat 10] Open questions: whose skull, who sent it, why. Re-hook: the candle going out.

**R2. The honest gift**
"A witch has just told a girl she is not welcome in her house. Then she pulls a skull off her fence, sets it on a pole, and says, here is the fire you came for. She means every word."
[beats 9-10] Open: what the fire does. Weaker picture than R1, better line.

**R3. Everybody knows where the fire is**
"Every fire in the house goes out tonight, and the last candle is pinched by a stepsister. A child is sent for a light. Nobody at that table needs to ask where the nearest one is."
[beat 1] Open: who walks, who planned it. Dread without any witch yet; the cold-open version of H1.

**R4. The question she keeps**
"'Ask about what you saw outside,' says Baba Yaga. 'Ask about what you saw inside, and I eat you.' The girl has just watched three pairs of hands carry grain through a kitchen. She has one question left, and she does not ask it."
[beats 7-8] Open: what the question was. Best use of the rule, now with a scene.

**R5. The wrong witch**
"Baba Yaga eats children. In the most famous story about her, a child walks into her house alone, in the dark, with her mother's doll in her pocket, and the witch sends her home with a present."
[beats 2, 9-10] Open: why. Plainest of the five; it names the assumption and breaks it in one line. No "not X but Y".

**R6. Three riders**
"A girl walking to a witch's house is passed by a rider in white, then a rider in red. The third rider is in black, and when he goes by, it is night, and the fence ahead of her has eyes."
[beats 3-4] Open: whose riders. Pure picture; keeps the tale-told-straight feel.

## Picks
1. **R1** for the picture and the ending glimpse. It also matches the title "Never Touched Her": the viewer sees the ash and wonders who actually did it.
2. **R4** as the backup, for the strangest unguessable detail in the tale.
