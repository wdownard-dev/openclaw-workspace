# HOOKS.md: 8 hooks for Baba Yaga, first 15 seconds

Voice per `../VOICE.md`: calm, dry, no hype words, no em dashes, at most one question, the hook opens a question and answers none. Word counts are spoken words (15 s at 150 wpm is 35-40). Source per hook in brackets. None uses the hut-description opening, the "she is more than a witch" thesis or "who is she really?" from Slavique (see REF-SLAVIQUE.md).

**H1. Cold open: the candle** (40 words)
"Every fire in the house is out but one candle. The stepsister leans over it and pinches the flame. Then the stepmother says, quite calmly, that somebody should walk into the forest for a light."
[Afanasyev, Vasilisa the Beautiful] Next beat: who walks.

**H2. Tally: nobody touched** (38 words) **TOP 2**
"In the best-known Baba Yaga story, three people burn to ashes before morning. The witch never touches any of them. All she does is give a visitor exactly what the visitor came for."
[Afanasyev, ending; beat 10] Next beat: the candle. Mechanism: number plus a vague, exciting glimpse of the ending (VOICE.md allows it); the viewer wants to know how.

**H3. The rule: two kinds of question** (40 words) **TOP 2**
"You may ask Baba Yaga about what you saw outside her house. You may not ask about what you saw inside. She eats people who ask the second kind of question, and she says so herself."
[Afanasyev/Wheeler, Vasilisa; beat 8] Next beat: the girl who has to decide which kind to ask.

**H4. Picture: three riders** (38 words)
"On the road to Baba Yaga's house, a girl is passed by three riders. One is all in white, on a white horse. One is all in red. By the time the third one passes, it is night."
[Afanasyev, Vasilisa; beat 3-4] Next beat: the black rider and what lights up behind him.

**H5. Object: the doll** (36 words)
"A dying mother hands her daughter a doll and two instructions. Feed it. Tell no one. The daughter obeys both, and that is the reason a witch with a fence of skulls cannot keep her."
[Afanasyev/Wheeler, beats 2, 9] Next beat: why the girl is walking into the forest.

**H6. You: eight years old** (40 words)
"You are eight years old. It is dark, and you have been sent for fire. The nearest fire belongs to a woman who rides through the air in a mortar and has a very fixed idea about visitors."
[Afanasyev/Wheeler, beats 2, 5 (age eight (v))] Next beat: the stepmother, and how you got here.

**H7. The odd question: the sender** (38 words)
"Who sends an eight-year-old into a forest at night to ask a witch for fire? A stepmother with a plan. The plan works. It just works on everyone except the girl."
[Afanasyev, beats 1, 10] The one question in the hook. Next beat: the plan.

**H8. Detail: the hands** (39 words)
"Three pairs of hands appear in Baba Yaga's yard and carry off the grain. The girl watching has a question about that. She keeps it to herself, and that silence is why she goes home alive."
[Afanasyev/Wheeler, beats 7-8] Hands "with no arms" is ours and is not used. Next beat: how she got into the yard.

## Top 2 and why
1. **H2.** It is the twist in one image: the viewer expects a child-eating witch and is told nobody is touched. It has a number and stakes inside 10 s, a glimpse of the ending (allowed in a hook per William 2026-11-14) and a clear next question, how. Packaging fit: it is the same claim as the title "Never Touched Her".
2. **H3.** The most unguessable line in the tale, in a calm second-person voice, with a rule the viewer will want to see tested. It sets up beats 8-9, the middle of the story, rather than the ending, so the two top hooks pay off at different points. Backup picture hook if William wants the scene first: H4.

Re-hook (0:30-1:00, a story beat, not a meta line): the stepsister's candle has gone out and the forest is the only place left with a fire.
