## Hook flags (raised separately; hook not changed)
1. **B-001 "most famous".** No locator exists and none can be built from the texts. Wikipedia's Baba Yaga article lists "Vasilisa the Beautiful" among the tales (and a 1940 film, and Bilibin's 1900 illustrations) but does not call it the most famous. It cannot be shown from a primary source, so by the stance rule it is an unproven superlative. **Ruling: it can stay only as William's editorial claim; the nearest defensible wording is "One of the best-known Baba Yaga stories ends with a family burned to ashes."** ("One of the best-known" is not a hedge: it is a flat statement. Support: the 1940 Soviet film titled after it and Bilibin's 1900 illustrations (both on Wikipedia's Baba Yaga page), Wheeler's 1912 collection giving it a tale of its own, and Ralston's own praise, "one of the most spirited and dramatic of all the Skazkas" (R p.149) and "one of the best folk-tales I know" (R p.158 note). None of that proves "most".) Not applied; William's call.
2. **B-001 "ends with".** In A and W the tale goes on after the burning (linen, the tsar, the wedding); R prints the burning as the climax and puts the rest in a bracketed epitome ("what follows is inferior in dramatic interest", R p.158 note), and the script itself narrates the sequel at B-126. "Ends with" is true of the dramatic ending and of R's printed ending, not of the whole tale. If William wants zero risk: "climaxes with". Not applied.
3. **B-002 "a stepmother sends".** The stepmother engineers it (puts out the fires, orders the snuffing) and the stepsisters send the girl out (W p.37-38; A; R p.152). Holds as worded; flagged only so the NOTES wording "the sisters push her out" stays the reference answer.

B-001 "burned to ashes" itself checks (W p.53; A has coal, R cinders).
