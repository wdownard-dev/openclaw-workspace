# Editor review of the v2 upgrade test (Cyclops), 2026-11-12

William's verdict: "very shaky and kind of childish". Reviewed `pipeline/remotion-v2/out.mp4` (76.5 s, 1080p/30, 20 shots) from frames and numbers, not by watching. Nothing in the project was modified; scratch work is in /tmp/rv2 and copies of the measuring scripts are in `/home/williamdownard/.openclaw/workspace-editor/bluepill-v2-review-tools/`.

Labels: [V] verified from the render or source, [E] estimate or judgement, [U] unverified.

**Short version.** Both complaints are real and have different causes.
- "Shaky" is mostly self-inflicted and cheap to fix. It comes from the impact shakes (an 11 to 15 Hz sine sampled at 30 fps, so it aliases into random jumps of 50+ px) plus under-damped bounce springs and idle motion on every layer.
- "Childish" comes from a stack of cartoon-for-kids signals, and the round-head character is only one of them. Motion grammar, type, grade, reaction emoji and sound are the rest. I recommended several of these in my last review, so I own part of it (section 4).

## 1. "Shaky": what was measured and what causes it

### Method [V]
- Decoded all 2,295 frames at 320x180 grey with Remotion's bundled ffmpeg. Estimated the global frame-to-frame shift (±14 px search at 320 px, so ±84 px at 1080p, sub-pixel by parabola fit). Also estimated separate shifts for the top band (mostly background) and the bottom band (mostly characters).
- Spot-checked consecutive full frames visually (boulder impact, opening entrance, ship).
- Computed spring overshoot from Remotion's own `spring()` for the configs in the source.
- Limits: this is a crude global-motion estimate, and parallax and moving characters contaminate it. Values at the ±84 px cap are saturated whip transitions, not shake. Treat the numbers as order of magnitude.

### What the numbers say
**Baseline motion is calm.** Outside events, camera speed is 0.1 to 1 px/frame at 1080p, or about 3 to 30 px/s [V]. That is slow. The push-ins, pans and idle bob are not what feels shaky. Shots like `hiding` (0.16 px/frame), `end` (0.09), `map` (0.15) and `wine` (0.36) are steady.

**The shake events are violent.** Frame-to-frame shift during impacts [V]:

| Time | Shot | Peak frame-to-frame shift | Frames | Sign pattern |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 19.77-20.13 s | owner, Cyclops lands | up to 56 px (x), 31 px (y) | 12 | alternates almost every frame |
| 23.93-24.37 s | boulder lands | 47 to 59 px, x alternating +47 / -59 / +47 / -57 / +38 | 11 | alternates every frame |
| 17.40-17.63 s | "MISTAKE" | 59 px | 6 | alternates |
| 37.70-37.87 s | "NOBODY" | 28 px | 4 | alternates |
| 2.03-2.40 s | "SIX" | 42 px | 9 | alternates |
| 45.97-46.17 s | flash into "BLIND" | 59 px | 7 | alternates |
| 59.4-59.7 s | ship slides in (spring overshoot) | 48 to 59 px | 8 | back and forth |

- Shake plus the tail of the whips: 18 events and 85 frames above 10 px/frame, about 2.8 s of the 76.5 s, in 7 shake bursts (plus 4 whips and 2 flashes) [V].
- Per-shot rms of the second difference of the shift ("jerk") is 0.04 to 0.5 px in calm shots, against 15 to 34 px in `owner`, `boulder`, `mistake`, `nobody` and `hook-six` [V]. That is a 30 to 100 times difference.
- The jolts also rotate the whole frame. The boulder frames show about 2° of roll [V, visible in the consecutive stills].

### Root causes in the source [V]

**1. `camera.ts` `shakeAt()` is the main culprit.**
```
sx = sin(u*71) * a * env      // 71 rad/s = 11.3 Hz
sy = cos(u*97) * a*0.8 * env  // 97 rad/s = 15.4 Hz
sr = sin(u*53) * a*90 * env   // 8.4 Hz, degrees
env = (1 - u/d)^2 ; a = 0.015-0.026 ; d = 0.5-0.7 s
```
- Amplitude: `a` is a fraction of the frame. 0.02 means 38 px, and 0.026 (boulder) means 50 px. The shake is 5 to 10 times larger than what reads as a "thud" on screen. Rotation `a*90` gives 1.4° to 2.3°, which shifts the corners by about 30 px more.
- Frequency: 71 rad/s sampled at 30 fps is 2.4 rad per frame, and the Nyquist limit is 94 rad/s. The 15 Hz y-term is above Nyquist (97 rad/s), so it aliases. The shake looks like random jumps, not a rumble. That matches the alternating sign pattern in the table.
- Duration: 0.5 to 0.7 s, with a squared envelope that holds high amplitude for the first 4 to 6 frames.
- Different layers shake by different amounts (bg 0.6x of the shake with 0.55 parallax, fg 1.4x), so the layers tear against each other during the shake, on top of the shake itself.

**2. The impact words shake too** (`Impact` in Scene.tsx). Position jitter of `sin(u*83)*14 + cos(u*61)*10` px (13 Hz, aliased the same way) and an 11 to 13 Hz scale spring (damping 9, stiffness 240, mass 0.6). That spring has a damping ratio of 0.38, peaks at 1.28 and so undershoots the text scale to 55% after the slam. The text is already bouncing while the screen is shaking.

**3. Under-damped bounce on every entrance** (`Cutout` spring `{damping 10, stiffness 120, mass 0.8}`):
- Damping ratio 0.51, peak 1.155 at 0.30 s, still outside ±2% until 0.63 s [V computed].
- With `enterDist` 0.5 to 0.7 of frame width, the overshoot is 15.5% of 960 to 1,344 px, which is 150 to 208 px past the mark and back. The ship (0.7) overshoots by about 208 px. This is the 48 to 59 px/frame wobble at 59.4 s.
- A walk hop on top: `|sin(t*11)| * 0.018 * H` is 19 px at 3.5 hops/s while travelling in. Characters that slide in as flat sprites and hop like a toy is a bouncy-toy signal.
- Marks (`!`, `?`, sweat) use damping 8, stiffness 220, mass 0.5: ratio 0.38, overshoot 27% [V].

**4. Everything is always moving.** The default bob is 6 px at 0.85 Hz plus 0.8% breathing on every cutout, each with its own phase, on top of a slow push-in on every shot. Individually mild (about 1 px/frame), but the screen never settles. In the busiest shots there are 5 to 8 simultaneous motion sources: `owner` has push-in, a giant entrance with 178 px overshoot, thud shake, name plate, hero hop, `!` mark, bob and whoosh [V from source]. [E] this is why it reads as "shaky" even where nothing literally shakes.

**5. Layer parallax makes feet slide on the ground.** `K = {bg: z 0.85, p 0.55, char: z 1, fg: z 1.25}`. During a push-in the background zooms less than the characters, so a character's feet creep across the painted floor. At a 1.16 push-in with the focus 400 px from the feet, that is about 16 px of slide over the shot [E]. It is slow, so it reads as "floaty", not "jittery". It is the flat-sticker-on-painting problem in motion.

**6. Transitions.** 4 whips (a 100% width slide with 14 px blur in 7 frames, each with a whoosh) and 2 white flashes at 0.9 alpha (the flash into "BLIND", the storm lightning) [V]. Full-frame lateral slides at this speed add to the feeling that the video lurches.

**7. Pacing.** 15.7 shots/min with 6 shots at 2.4 s or less and a mean of 3.8 s [V, V2-NOTES]. Not a cause of shake, but fast cuts plus bounce is "frantic".

### Fixes with target values

| Parameter | Now | Target |
|---|---|---|
| Camera shake amplitude | 0.015 to 0.026 of frame (29 to 50 px) | 0.003 to 0.005 (6 to 10 px) |
| Shake rotation | `a*90` = 1.4° to 2.3° | 0 |
| Shake frequency | 8 to 15 Hz sines | one kick, 2 to 3 Hz, decaying: `A*exp(-u*10)*sin(2π*3*u)`, over ≥ 7 frames per cycle |
| Shake duration | 0.5 to 0.7 s | 0.25 to 0.3 s |
| Shake count | 7 (plus 6 impact-word shakes) | at most 3 per video (boulder, blind, one more); the rest are hard cuts plus sound |

| Camera speed | 0.2 to 1 px/frame (fine) | keep ≤ 0.6 px/frame; never combine zoom with a pan |
| Bg/char parallax | bg zoom 0.85x, pan 0.55x | same zoom and pan for bg and characters (feet stay on the ground); parallax only for sky or far layers |
| Whips | 4 (100% slide, 14 px blur) | 0 to 1; use cuts and 8 to 12 frame dissolves |
| Flash | 0.9 alpha | 0.3 to 0.4, or none |

Assumption for the numbers above: [E] the targets come from standard motion-design practice, not from testing this footage. I could not judge how they feel in real time (see section 6).

## 2. "Childish": causes, and whether the round head is the problem

### What reads as childish [V = visible in stills, E = judgement]
1. **Character rendering.** [V] Blank round heads with dot eyes and a permanent small smile on every figure, including a Cyclops who is about to eat six men. Thick uniform black outline, rounded toy proportions (head about a third of the body), glossy flat fills. This looks like a preschool app mascot, not a deadpan stick-figure. The tonal clash (smiling mascots, "Six never walk out") reads as unintentional, not ironic.
2. **Only one pose per character.** [V, V2-NOTES weakness 1] Every figure is the same front-facing sprite, so acting is done with emoji-style marks (`!`, `?`, sweat drop, an X over the eye, `zzz`). That vocabulary is kids' cartoon and manga reaction language.
3. **Motion grammar.** [V] Overshoot, hop, squash, wobble, spring-pop entrances, and shake. It is all cartoon-bounce.
4. **Type.** [V] Bangers (comic-book) for impact words, Lilita One (rounded, friendly) for captions, both with heavy 10 px ink strokes and drop shadows, in yellow and red. Together they read as a children's comic or game-show caption.
5. **Palette and grade.** [V] Almost every shot is warm orange or pink sunset with a yellow/orange multiply grade; the few cool shots (stake night, end card) are the strongest and read as more serious. There is very little contrast range between shots.
6. **Cutouts on paintings.** [V] Flat vector stickers on painterly backgrounds. The style mismatch is the thing that says "pasted on", and the shadow and tint added in v2 only partly hide it. The ship cutout also has visible matte residue: a dotted "marching ants" outline around the sail, and the oar ports show the sea through as holes [V, crop of 62.2 s].
7. **Narration register.** [E] Short, simple declaratives ("His crew is starving", "The Cyclops loves it", "That is their first mistake", "The plan works"), mean sentence about 10 words. Read by a British storyteller voice this sounds like a bedtime story. The 156 wpm pace is fine; the vocabulary and the sing-song cadence are what read young. I did not hear it [U].
8. **Audio.** [U] Synthesised A-minor pad with frame drum and plucks, synthesised pops, dings and whooshes, a thud on every impact word. Pop and ding are the standard "kid-show" sound effects. I cannot hear it, so this is a risk, not a finding.
9. **Impact-word habit.** [V] Six full-screen words ("SIX", "MISTAKE", "NOBODY", "BLIND!", "ODYSSEUS!", "PRIDE") at 210 to 360 px, with sfx. That is a lot of shouting in 76 s.

### Honest comparison to the reference channels [V from REF and OUTLIER notes, otherwise E]
- **Unknown Frequencies uses the same basic character**: round white heads, dot eyes, tiny mouths (REF). Odyssey 1.67M views, 92k subs. So the round head is not a disqualifier for this audience, and William has said he likes that look.
- What differs, as far as the notes show:
  - Their figures are described as "simple" and hand-made (Paint); ours are polished vector with thick outlines. Crude plus deadpan reads as irony. Polished plus smiling reads as a kids' brand. [E]
  - REF records hand-lettered title cards in one font and one accent colour, plus arrows and counters. It does not record camera shake, bounce, comic fonts or six exploding words. [U] I have not watched the channel, only read the notes, so I cannot say it has none.
  - Its cuts are about 12.6 per minute (164 cuts in 13 min) but, from the REF notes, they are cuts between stills and cutaways, not moving sprites. We pair 15.7 cuts/min with motion on every layer. [E]
  - The REF narration text tone was not recorded in our notes [U]. The one reference transcript I read (Paint Explainer, a different channel, in projects/future/format-refs) is casual and plain but uses longer sentences with a deadpan aside ("it was pretty weird", "that alone would be crazy enough, but it gets stranger") [V], more adult than "The plan works".
- OUTLIER-CHECK shows the niche rewards several looks: flat cartoons (Hard Times in Hellas, Odrin, Cryptic Chronicles), stylised illustrated or AI-looking art (Mythological Man, Mr L, MythLore Unpacked) and gory painterly thumbnails (Mr. Discovery) [V]. "Stick-figure / animated" titles specifically had a lower hit rate (median 58k, 16 videos, none from small channels), but that keyword is polluted by gaming and fan animation [V from the note itself].
- **Verdict [E]:** the round head is a contributor, not the root. The root is that we built a polished mascot style and animated it with kids'-cartoon motion, type and sound. The same cast, with calm motion, a deadpan face, cooler grade and restrained type, would read considerably older. How much older, I cannot say without a test.
- **Caveat:** William's reaction is n=1. He is a stand-in for the audience, not the audience.

## 3. Ranked changes, and the next test

### Top 5 by impact (confidence in brackets)
1. **Replace the shake and the impact-word shake** with the single 2 to 3 Hz decaying kick (6 to 10 px, no rotation, 0.25 to 0.3 s) or a 3% zoom punch, one value for every layer, and limit it to 3 per video. Removes the 7 shake bursts, about 9 of the 18 measured events above 10 px (the rest are whips, flashes and entrance overshoot, covered by changes 2 and the whip row). [V cause, E target values]
2. **Kill the bounce vocabulary**: entrance spring to damping 26 / stiffness 120 / mass 1, entrance distance ≤ 0.25 W or none, no walk hop, bob ≤ 2 px on the speaker only, drop `wobble` and `squash`, marks fade in instead of springing. Fixes "floaty" and most of "toy". [V cause, E targets]
3. **Re-tone the surface**: one serif or condensed type family (small, thin stroke) instead of Bangers and Lilita One; one big impact word per chapter at most, not six; cooler grade with real contrast range (the stake and end-card shots are the model); same zoom for bg and characters; remove `?` `!` sweat marks; fix the ship matte. [V problems, E result]
4. **Narration and sound**: write the script for adults (longer sentences, specifics, a dry aside or two, the sources the Strategist asked for), direct the voice slower and flatter, and replace the synthesised music and sound effects with licensed tracks and real foley (rumble, stone, wind), with no pop or ding. [E, U on audio since I cannot hear it]
5. **Character style test** (A and B above): deadpan faces and poses, or black-figure silhouettes. Highest ceiling, highest cost, least certain. Do it as the A/B below, not as a commitment.

### Proposed A/B (cheap, one afternoon)
Take the cave sequence, shots 4 to 9 (10.03 to 36.2 s, 26 s), with the same narration and the same backgrounds so only the treatment varies.
- **A (control):** current v2.
- **B (calm, same cast):** change 1, 2 and 3 only. Same assets, no new images. Build is about 1 to 2 hours of parameter changes in `camera.ts`, `Scene.tsx` and `theme.ts`. This isolates "is it the motion and surface?".
- **C (silhouette):** same motion preset as B, with the black-figure set: 1 terracotta cave background, Odysseus (profile, two poses), Polyphemus (profile, club), boulder, sheep, crew silhouettes. About 12 image calls ($0.5 to $1 [E]) plus 2 to 3 hours.
- Render each (about 26 s, so roughly 30 s per render by the 77 s / 76 s ratio in V2-NOTES). Keep audio constant in this round (same narration, same bed), so the A/B/C difference is visual. Do audio as a second test.
- **Objective check:** run `bluepill-v2-review-tools/motion.js` plus `analyze14.js` on each render. Pass means jerk rms ≤ 2 px and no frame-to-frame shift above 15 px outside transitions.
- **Subjective check:** William watches B and C blind in random order and answers three questions: which feels more shaky, which feels more like it is for adults, which he would keep watching. If B closes most of the gap, the round head is fine. If C wins clearly, the style is the problem. Either answer is useful; neither is audience data. Real evidence is CTR and average view duration after upload.
- **Do not decide from this alone:** n=1 viewer, 26 s, and the unheard audio.

## 4. What I got wrong in my last review (REVIEW-2026-11-12.md), exposed by this build
1. **"Cutout entrances with overshoot, bob, simple parallax"** was my recommendation, and v2 built it literally. Overshoot, bob and mismatched parallax produced the bounce, the float and the sliding feet. I gave no amplitude or damping, so the default springs came out toy-like. Correct advice: critically damped, small, speaker-only.
2. **"One big hand-lettered impact word per beat with pop and shake"** produced six shouting words and a shaking frame. Pop and shake are the two things to remove. One per chapter at most.
3. **"Sound: whooshes, thuds, ducked music bed, chapter stings"** produced synthesised kid-show effects (pop, ding, whoosh) I have not heard. I should have specified real recorded foley and licensed music, and said whether to use impact sounds at all.
4. **"Hard cut on punchlines"** was fine in itself, but I paired it with 11 to 13 shots/min and, implicitly, "make it move". The result is 15.7 shots/min with motion on every layer. The reference's rate of 12.6 cuts/min is cuts between mostly static images [E, from notes], which is a very different load. I should have said "cut faster only if you move less".
5. **"Cutout reuse for most shots"** (80 to 100 unique images, the rest cutout reuse) assumed reuse is free. With one front-facing pose per character it reads as a puppet show and is the main reason acting is carried by emoji marks. Poses are not a polish item; they are a prerequisite for the cutout approach.
6. **I praised "clean warm palette" and "character consistency"** and treated the round-head style as settled because William liked the reference. This build shows the polished, smiling, thick-outline version does not carry the reference's tone, and a uniformly warm sunset palette is a weakness. I asked for ground shadows and tint on cutouts; they were added and the sticker-on-painting look remains, because the cause is the style gap and not the lighting.
7. **I did not flag the fonts.** Bangers and Lilita One were not in my review and are part of the childish read.
8. **I never separated what made the reference look credible from what we could easily build.** The reference is crude, deadpan and (per the notes) mostly static; our pipeline makes polished, animated sprites. Those pull in opposite directions, and I should have said so when I set the upgrade list.

## 5. Verified vs not (summary)
- [V] Shake bursts, their sizes and the alternating sign pattern; the source parameters that cause them; spring overshoot numbers (computed with Remotion's `spring()`); mean shot length, cut rate, whip and flash counts; ship matte residue; font and palette choices; narration text.
- [E] The target values, the claim that the shake is what reads as "shaky", the claim that the narration register and sound add to "childish", the cost and drift ratings of the alternatives, the sliding-feet estimate.
- [U] Audio entirely; whether Unknown Frequencies uses any bounce or shake; real-time motion feel; how the black-figure alternative would be received in this niche; 2.5D feasibility on CPU.

## 6. What I could not judge
- **Audio**: music, effects, voice prosody and mix. Everything said about sound is a risk, not a finding.
- **Real-time motion feel**: how 30 fps playback, display refresh and platform compression make the shake and bounce feel. Numbers show the jumps are large and aliased; they do not say how annoying they are at speed.
- **William's specific trigger**: I do not know which moments he found shaky. The boulder, owner and ship entrance are the likely ones; confirm with him.
- **The reference channel itself**: I read notes and one transcript, not footage.
