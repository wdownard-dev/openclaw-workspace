# RESEARCH-V1.md: the Cyclops, myth versus reality (Project Blue Pill, video 1)

Researched 2026-11-12. Nothing generated, uploaded or sent. Feeds `SCRIPT-V1.md` and `SHOTLIST-V1.md`.

Labels (William's scheme):
- **[V]** verified in a primary text I read this session, or in a reputable secondary source I read, which I name and link.
- **[E]** scholarly inference or common retelling. Say it with "may", "one theory", "scholars think".
- **[U]** not verified. Do not say it.

How I checked: I read Homer Odyssey 9 and 1.68-75 in two English translations and searched the Greek text; read Hesiod, Euripides (Shelley), Theocritus (Lang), Thucydides, Virgil and Pausanias in public-domain translations; read Wikipedia articles that cite the modern scholarship; read Abel's 1914 passage in Jason Colavito's translation; and read Mayor's own comments in Discover Magazine.

**What I could not read:** (1) Witton and Hing 2024 (publisher bot check blocked the paper; I did not try to get round it). (2) Aguirre and Buxton 2020 (paywalled). (3) Mayor's book text (archive.org lending copy is locked). (4) Abel's original German. The script uses paraphrase, not verbatim quotation, for Witton and Hing and for Aguirre and Buxton, and `SCRIPT-V1.md` lists the read-before-publish checks.

Wikipedia is used only where it cites a named scholar, and the citation is given. It is a pointer, not the authority.

**Translation caveats.** "Noman" (Butler, Murray), "Nobody" (modern translations) and "No-one" are English choices for *Outis*; the pun on *mē tis* ("not anyone") and *mētis* ("cunning") exists only in Greek [E]. The size of the club and the length of the stake vary by translator. ## 2. Confidence ladder

**Say flatly (verified in primary text or in more than one reliable source)**
- What Homer's Book 9 says: the cave, the stone (twenty-two wagons), six men eaten across three meals, the olive stake, the strong wine, "Noman/Nobody", the other Cyclopes' conclusion, the escape under the rams, the taunt, Telemus's prophecy, the hurled hilltop, the prayer to Poseidon, and Poseidon's resulting grudge (A4 to A17).
- Book 9 never writes "one eye" or "forehead" (A13), with the scope "in Book 9".
- Hesiod names Brontes, Steropes and Arges and says one eye sat in their foreheads (B1). Euripides and Theocritus say one eye too (B4, B5).
- Thucydides lists the Cyclopes among Sicily's earliest inhabitants and says he cannot say what race they were (D1); the Aeolian forge belief (D2).
- Pausanias says Cyclopes built the walls of Tiryns and Mycenae (D4); archaeologists still say "cyclopean masonry" (D5).
- Dwarf elephants lived on Sicily, Malta, Crete, Cyprus and several Aegean islands; the Sicilian species stood under a metre; their skulls have a large central nasal opening (C9, C10, C2).
- Abel proposed the skull idea in 1914 (C1); Mayor has said she cannot prove it (C7); the Empedocles story has no surviving source (C4).

**Hedge ("may", "one theory", "some scholars", "unproven")**
- That elephant skulls inspired the Cyclops: **a plausible hypothesis, not proven** (C1, C5, C7).
- Mayor's broader claim that fossils fed giant and monster myths (C6): "she argues".
- That the blinding tale is older than Homer: "most scholars think" (C13).
- That the smith Cyclopes reflect metalworking guilds (B9): "suggested ... may".
- That volcanoes explain the forge-under-the-mountain idea (D3): "helps".
- Aci Trezza: "local tradition says" (D6).
- Anything attributed to Witton and Hing, or Aguirre and Buxton, until the papers are read: keep to paraphrase.

## 3. Claims NOT to make

## 4. Best verifiable beats for hooks and re-hooks

1. **Hook (verified, visual):** an elephant on Sicily under a metre tall, with one big hole in the middle of its skull (C10, C2). It is a real animal, a real fact, and sets up the question in 13 seconds.
2. **"Homer never writes the words 'one eye'."** Book 9 says "the eye" (A13). The explicit statement comes from Hesiod (B1). It is a surprise for most viewers and easy to demonstrate on screen, and the Editor can check it in two minutes (section 7).
3. **The most famous version of the theory leans on a story with no source.** Abel's Empedocles claim is unsupported (C4), and Mayor, the theory's best-known champion, says "we just can't prove that" (C7). It turns a debunk-or-confirm video into an honest "what can we say?" one.
4. **The stone is the trap.** Odysseus cannot kill the giant because twenty-two wagons could not move the door (A6, A7). It re-frames the cave as a puzzle and gives a mid-story hook.
5. **Thucydides declines to say.** He lists the Cyclopes, then says he cannot tell what race they were (D1); and **the folktale has two hundred versions from Iceland to Korea** (C13), so the story may not need a Sicilian skull at all. Use the second as the "what we can say" re-hook.

Honourable mentions: the wine mixed twenty to one (A10); Pausanias's mules and the walls (D4); the Cyclops talks to his ram (A15); Theocritus's lovesick Polyphemus (B5).

## 6. Where the common story is wrong or overstated
- "The Greeks knew the skulls were Cyclopes": no ancient evidence (C4, C7).
- "Mayor proved it": she explicitly does not claim that (C7).
- "Homer says one eye / Sicily": neither (A2, A13).
- "Cyclops means one-eyed": see B8.
- "Cyclopes are one race": Greek writers kept three distinct traditions (B3).
- The Cyclopia explanation, sometimes repeated: anatomically mismatched (C12).

## 7. How to repeat the "one eye" check (Editor, 2 minutes)
