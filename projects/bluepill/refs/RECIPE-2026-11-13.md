## 2. What all five do (and we do not) [E]

1. **Every frame is a painted scene.** No white cutaways at all. The background is a full, warm, AI-painted plate; the character stands inside it at ground scale, lit by it. Our 42% white is the single biggest gap, and our painted sprites "pasted" onto plates is the second.
2. **The character and the background are one picture.** These channels do not composite sprites. Each shot is one generated image with the stick figure already in it. That is why the figures sit naturally. Consistency comes from a character design so simple (round white head, dot eyes, one costume marker: helmet, cloak, topknot) that the model reproduces it every time.
3. **Change comes from cutting, not layering.** 13-16 cuts a minute, 2-4 pop-ins. The picture changes; things do not pile onto the picture. We did the opposite: 9.5 cuts, 6.5 pops, chips and stamps stacking up.
4. **On-screen text is rare and huge.** One-word cards ("REX", "DOOM", "LAW", "SYSTEM"), a name or place label when someone or somewhere is introduced ("ALEXANDER", "THEBES", "53 BC"), the odd diagram. Never small evidence chips. Our text share of 21.6% should drop to a few percent.
5. **One palette per video.** Rome: browns, golds, red. Japan: muted ukiyo-e blues and ochres. Alexander: sunny yellow and purple. The palette is the style; the plates can vary in detail as long as the palette holds.
6. **The story is told straight.** Chronological, one thread, named people doing things, concrete numbers, a twist. Not "was this real", not evidence and verdicts. William said this himself about Rome. Our Myth Ledger framing (sources, stamps, verdict chips, Greek line, Abel 1923) is what makes the narration feel like "too much is being said": every beat carries argument weight on top of story weight.
7. **Hook pattern (all five):** a bold claim with a number in the first sentence, two or three escalating facts, "and then it all collapsed" or "yet he never lost", then the question that the video answers. Thirty seconds, 8-10 shots, ends on the narrator figure or a map.
8. **Chapters.** Named chapters every 1.5-3 minutes, shown as a big card. Japan's 12 chapters at 974k views suggests the chapter rhythm helps retention on long runs.
9. **Transitions.** Hard cuts carry almost everything. Occasional slow push-in or pan on a plate (Stick Secrets does it on every shot). Two- or three-panel comic layouts for a sequence of actions. No wipes, no whoosh pops.

## 3. Where they differ (choose a lane)

- **Genius History / Genius Legends (Japan, Alexander):** calm. Long sentences (14-17 words), few sentences a minute, zero questions, almost zero pop-ins, zero white. Lets the pictures breathe. Highest views of the five.
- **Globa1 (Rome, Mongols):** punchier. Short sentences (8-10 words), more one-word text cards, diagrams, comparison panels, a narrator stick figure who addresses the camera. Still 1% white.
- **Stick Secrets (archers):** one topic question instead of a whole history, shortest shots, every plate slowly pans. Most "cinematic" of the five.

## 4. Recipe for us

**Script (V5):** tell the Cyclops story straight, in order, 6-8 named chapters. 150 wpm. Mean 10-12 words a sentence, 12-14 sentences a minute, 0.3-0.5 questions a minute. Hook per section 2.7. Keep Daniel's voice and settings. Move the "what's real" material to a 30-45 s coda or the description; keep at most three on-screen stamps in the whole video.

**Picture:** 0% white. One painted plate per shot, about 150-200 plates for 10-12 minutes, 13-16 cuts a minute, median shot 3.5-4 s, longest 8 s. Pop-ins under 3 a minute. Text: one-word cards, name and date labels, big. One locked palette (ink blue, verdigris and gold still work as a palette; drop the parchment stamps).

**Characters:** switch to the genre's simple figure (round head, dot eyes, costume marker) and generate them inside each plate, one image per shot, instead of compositing our detailed sprites. Our current Odysseus and Polyphemus sprites are better drawings than the references use, and that is the problem: they do not sit in the scene.

**Cost [E]:** 200 images at gpt-image-2.6 medium, about $0.04-0.10 each, so $8-20 per video. Compositing work drops to near zero. Remotion stays for cuts, text cards, maps and timing.

**Test before committing:** 10 shots from the Cyclops story as one-image-per-shot stick-figure plates in our palette (about $1), reviewed in Figma next to the current sprites.

## 5. Things to avoid (seen in the references)

- Rome's 3-panel comic frames at 2 s are too fast to read; use panels only on 4 s+ shots.
- Text share rises fast with Globa1-style diagrams; cap at one diagram per chapter.
- Stick Secrets pans every shot; on our hardware that is fine (Remotion), but constant motion makes the narration feel rushed. Use pans on 1 in 4 shots.

## 6. Continuity rules (added 2026-11-13 after the V5 audit: 59% of plates failed the sentence-match test)

1. **Scene-state ledger before any plate.** For every beat: location, time of day, door sealed/open, who is present and how many, action direction. State carries forward and changes only when the narration changes it. Inject the state line into every prompt. (`plates-v5/state.json`, built from the script.)
2. **Describe only who is present.** Never the full cast. Single-subject beats get "only this one figure". Never pass a cast sheet as an image reference: the model copies the line-up and ignores the shot.
3. **Unfilmable shot lines are script bugs.** Check each shot line against the state before generating (sky through a sealed door, "five figures" when eight remain, two giants merged into one frame).
4. **Sentence-match audit is a render gate.** View every plate at full size next to its line, the previous line and the next line. Fail on wrong location, door state, cast, count, direction, time, or an action that contradicts the next line. Re-roll with state-injected prompts, re-audit, max 2 rounds, then fix by hand. Zero fails before anyone sees the video.
5. **Cards land on the subject**, not the chapter boundary (`plates-v5/cards.json`).

## 7. Final production pipeline (locked 2026-11-14 after V7)
1. Topic + angle, one line. 2. Script in the calm lane (SCRIPT-V5 shape), shot line + scene state per beat. 3. Library: model sheet per character/place/prop, masters + derived variants, CAST-CHECK (0 missing), contradiction audit A-F, LOCKED.md. One labelled CARD per character (turnaround+face+expressions+name+identity line). 4. Scene plan: ~70 scenes per 10 min, base + variants + crops/pans, scale line per scene (crew 0.95, Polyphemus 4x). 5. Generation on Gemini 3.1 Flash Image, F1 edit framing (place plate image 1, one card per character present, identity lines verbatim, checklist repeated), 2 candidates per base, variants by "edit image 1, change only", exact-count crowds composited from a cream row. 6. Audit gate: identity checklist + continuity (line, prev, next, state) + scale; 0 fails per chapter before the next. 7. Remotion: hard cuts, push every 4th beat, no cards/bands. Cost ~$15-20 images per video. Lessons: no cast sheet as reference on OpenAI; crowds above 5 cannot be counted by the model; beard-size features vanish in wide shots (keep designs bold); test slice of 2:30 before the full run.

Rule (2026-11-14): thumbnails, banners and any other channel art go through the same card method as plates (labelled cards + identity lines as references, checklist audit). Never pass bare turnarounds.
