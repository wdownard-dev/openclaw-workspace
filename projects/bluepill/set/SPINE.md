# SPINE.md: Set, "The villain was a promotion gone wrong" (video 3, Stage 1, 2026-11-18)

Master promise (raised in the hook, paid at beat 21): the god who killed Osiris also guards the sun every night, and Egypt's own politics are why we remember only the murder. Format: told straight, then where it came from (Cyclops model). Source traditions per beat. Dialogue and wording are ours and condensed. Locators marked (v) are for the fact-check stage to confirm; they never appear in narration as hedges.

## Beats (cold open to close)
**Cold open: the murder (about 1:30)**
1. A banquet. Set arrives with 72 conspirators and a painted chest cut to the exact measure of Osiris, and says whoever fits can keep it. (Plutarch, *Isis and Osiris* 13)
2. Osiris lies down in it. The lid is nailed shut, sealed with molten lead, and the chest is thrown into the Nile. (Plutarch 13)
3. Isis recovers the body. Set finds it while hunting boar by moonlight and cuts it into 14 pieces, scattered across Egypt. (Plutarch 18)
4. The oldest account is shorter: Set knocks Osiris down, and the Pyramid Texts say so, 2,000 years before Plutarch. (Pyramid Texts, Utterance 219 and 477 (v))

**The turn: same god, other job (about 2:30)**
5. Every night Ra's sun-barque sails through the underworld, and Set stands at its prow and spears Apophis, the serpent that tries to eat the sun. (Amduat, 7th hour; Book of the Dead vignettes of the barque (v))
6. Set is Ra's strongest defender because he is the strongest god: storm, thunder, the desert, the red land. (Chester Beatty I, Contendings of Horus and Set; Book of the Dead)
7. The trial: Horus and Set argue for the throne before the gods and the tribunal sits for 80 years without a verdict. (Chester Beatty I, Contendings)
8. The contests: Set's stone boat sinks, Horus's wooden boat is plastered to look like stone and floats; both turn into hippos and stay under water. (Chester Beatty I, Contendings)
9. Neith writes the letter that settles it: give the throne to Horus, double Set's property, and give him two foreign goddesses, Anat and Astarte, as wives. (Chester Beatty I, Neith's letter, Contendings)
10. The verdict: Horus is king of Egypt. Ra takes Set into the barque as his son, to thunder in the sky, and the gods call this fair. (Chester Beatty I, Contendings)
11. Pharaohs knew it. Seti I and Seti II carry the god in their names: Seti means "man of Set." Seti's son is Ramesses the Great. (Nineteenth Dynasty royal titularies)
12. Ramesses II marches to Kadesh in 1274 BC with four divisions: Amun, Ra, Ptah and Set. (Kadesh Poem and Bulletin)

**The why: where the villain came from (about 4:00)**
13. Set is the god of the Red Land, the desert, and everything outside the Nile valley, including foreigners and their storms. He is lord of Ombos in Upper Egypt, older than the pyramids. (Pyramid Texts; Naqada-period cult at Ombos)
14. About 2700 BC King Peribsen puts Set on his serekh in place of Horus. His successor Khasekhemwy puts both gods on his, Horus and Set together, to show the two halves of Egypt joined. (Second Dynasty serekh inscriptions)
15. The Hyksos, foreign kings from the Levant, rule the north from Avaris, and their king Apepi worships Set alone. His name is Apophis in Greek, the serpent Set spears. (Papyrus Sallier I, Quarrel of Apophis and Seqenenre)
16. Egypt drives the Hyksos out and the Ramessids rebuild Avaris, and Set stays the god of Avaris: on Ramesses II's Year 400 Stela he wears the dress of the Canaanite storm god Baal. (Year 400 Stela, Tanis)
17. Then the conquerors come back. Assyria sacks Thebes in 663 BC. Persia takes Egypt in 525 BC. The god of foreigners becomes the god of the people who just invaded. (Assyrian annals of Ashurbanipal; Herodotus 3)
18. Set's name is chiselled off monuments, his bronzes are melted, and his sign in names is rewritten. At Abydos Seti I's own name is carved with the Osiris sign, not the Set animal. (Late Period temple reliefs; Temple of Seti I, Abydos (v))

**Close (about 1:30)**
19. By the time Plutarch writes, the red-haired, donkey-coloured god of the desert is Typhon, and at Coptos they throw donkeys off a cliff. (Plutarch 30)
20. The twist: the banquet, the chest and the 72 men are in none of the Egyptian texts. Plutarch, a priest at Delphi, writes them down around AD 120. The murderer who hosts a dinner is the newest layer of Set. (Plutarch 13; Pyramid Texts for the Egyptian version) [pays the master promise's second half]
21. Payoff: the god who went from guarding the sun, to naming kings, to being scratched off walls, was built into a villain over about 2,000 years, partly by invaders and partly by a Greek storyteller. The murder was the last part added. (synthesis of beats 5, 11, 17-20) [pays the master promise]

## Research notes for the fact-check stage (not narration-facing)
Version picks (STANCE RULE) and why:
- **Chest vs Pyramid Texts murder**: told the Plutarch version (chest, 72 conspirators, 14 pieces) because it is the story with a picture. Plutarch 13 and 18 give 72 and 14; Egyptian temple lists give 14 or 16 or 42 pieces; picked 14. The twist at beat 20 turns on the Egyptian record containing a killing but no chest.
- **Set in the barque**: the spear-in-the-prow image is the New Kingdom and later tradition; picked it over the Pyramid Texts' Set as a threat to the king. Locator needs confirming (Amduat 7th hour has Isis and Set in the barque's company; the clear Set-spears-Apophis vignettes are New Kingdom and Late Period temple and Book of the Dead imagery).
- **Contendings ending**: picked Chester Beatty I's reconciliation (Set beside Ra as thunder) over Plutarch's Isis letting Set go (Plutarch 19) because it fits the promotion arc and is the Egyptian text. The "Neith's letter" gift of Anat and Astarte is in the same papyrus (Ramesside, c. 1150 BC).
- **Date of the trial**: "80 years" is Chester Beatty I (the court laments how long it has sat). Keep.
- **Seti I at Abydos**: picked the claim that his name is written with the Osiris sign (Wsir) in his Abydos temple; confirm against a source before it goes in the script, or cut to "his name is rewritten in later reliefs."
- **Erasure after 525 BC**: picked a single clear story (invasions, then erasure). The real pattern is spread over the Third Intermediate and Late Periods and the Persian-period Hibis temple still shows Set spearing Apophis; if the fact-check cannot back "chiselled out after the Persians" for a named monument, restate as "from the Late Period."
- **Hyksos Apepi**: Papyrus Sallier I (Nineteenth Dynasty copy of an earlier story): "King Apophis chose Sutekh as his lord." Apepi / Apophis spelling: Greek form of the royal name; the serpent Apep is the same word. The irony is accurate as a name coincidence, no claim of intent.
- **Persian and Assyrian dates**: Ashurbanipal's sack of Thebes 663 BC; Cambyses conquers Egypt 525 BC. Check against the sources.
- **Optional, cut by default**: the Contendings' crude episodes (Horus's seed on Set's lettuce, the severed hand) and the hippo contest are the strangest source details; keep the hippo, skip the rest for advertiser safety.
