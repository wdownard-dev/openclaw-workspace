# HOOKS.md: 8 hooks for Set (video 3), first 15 seconds

Voice per `../VOICE.md`: calm, dry, no hype words, no em dashes, at most one question, hook opens a question and answers none. Word counts are spoken words (about 15 s at 150 wpm = 35-40). Source per hook in brackets.

**H1. Scene: the chest** (36 words)
"A banquet. Seventy-two guests, and one painted chest cut to fit exactly one man. The host says whoever fits inside can keep it."
[Plutarch, *Isis and Osiris* 13] Next beat: Osiris lies down.

**H2. Contradiction: two jobs** (41 words)
"Set murdered his own brother and cut him into fourteen pieces. Every night after that, he stood at the front of the sun's boat and speared the serpent that wanted to eat the world. Egypt saw no problem with this."
[Plutarch 18; Amduat 7th hour] Next beat: the banquet.

**H3. Stat: the name** (36 words)
"Seti means 'man of Set.' Two pharaohs bore it, and one of them fathered Ramesses the Great. Set, the god who killed Osiris. Egypt named its kings after him."
[Nineteenth Dynasty titularies] Next beat: how the god of murder gets a king's name.

**H4. Question: the odd bodyguard** (38 words)
"Why would you trust your brother's murderer to guard the sun? Egypt did, for about a thousand years. Then, around 600 BC, someone started chiselling his name off the walls."
[Contendings; Late Period erasure] The one question in the hook.

**H5. Reversal: the verdict** (37 words)
"The gods held a trial that lasted eighty years. At the end, the accused murderer was not punished. He was given a seat beside the sun god, and two foreign goddesses as wives."
[Chester Beatty I, Contendings] Next beat: the crime itself.

**H6. Number-first: the fourth division** (37 words)
"In 1274 BC, Ramesses the Great marched to war with four divisions. Three were named for the gods of Egypt. The fourth was named for Set."
[Kadesh Poem and Bulletin] Next beat: who Set is and what he did.

**H7. Scene: the erasure** (35 words)
"A stonecutter stands at a temple wall with a chisel and a list. The list is of one god's names. He works down it, one cartouche at a time, until the god is gone."
[Late Period erasures (v)] Next beat: who he was.

**H8. Twist on the source: Greek storyteller** (40 words)
"The banquet, the chest and the seventy-two conspirators are the most famous crime in Egyptian myth. No Egyptian ever wrote them down. A Greek priest did, about two thousand years after the pyramids."
[Plutarch 13] Note: gives away the beat-20 twist, so use only if the video closes on a different payoff.

## Top 2
1. **H1 (the chest).** Pure picture, specific unguessable detail (a chest cut to one man's measure), leaves the next step unexplained, and it is the cold-open story, so no bridge line is needed. Per VOICE section 2, a hook that paints a picture beats one that narrates events (William chose "The sky is a skull" for this reason). It does not promise the twist, so it stays honest with the "promotion" angle, which arrives at beat 5.
2. **H5 (the verdict).** Raises the strongest open question (why was the murderer rewarded?) and hands the viewer a vague, exciting glimpse of the ending, which VOICE allows. Stakes in the first sentence, escalation in the third. The drawback: it plays the ending of the Contendings before the murder, so the story needs to start immediately with beat 1 as the answer to "what did he do?"

Runners-up: H2 (cleanest statement of the angle, best fit for the title) and H6 (best number-first opener, weak picture).
