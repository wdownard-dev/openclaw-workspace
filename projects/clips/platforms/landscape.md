# Clipping and UGC platforms similar to Vyro: landscape

Research date: 2026-10-24 (America/New_York). Method: public web only. No signups, logins, joins, submits, messages, or forms. Vyro's app (app.vyro.com) was not opened. Browser use was limited to my own tab, which I closed.

## Labels used

- **OFFICIAL**: a page on the platform's own domain that I read (help center, terms, FAQ, or homepage).
- **HEARSAY**: a third-party page or a vendor comparison. Vendor comparisons are marked VENDOR because the vendor ranks itself.
- **UNVERIFIED**: I could not read the platform's own pages, or I only saw a search snippet.
- Numbers are copied from the cited source. None are estimated.

## Fit criteria (William)

US-based individual. Posts through Buffer to TikTok and Instagram Reels. YouTube Shorts gets no distribution, so YouTube-only campaigns are low fit. English-language gaming and streamer content. Written permission from the rights holder is required before any clip. No gambling, crypto-casino, or mature content. Fit ratings below are my judgment from these criteria.

## Per-platform notes

### 1. Vyro (baseline)
- **Official (help center, updated 2026-08-21):** a creator must reach 5,000 views on each individual post before that post earns. The threshold is per post, not cumulative.
- **Official:** payouts run through Stripe or PayPal only. IBAN, ACH, and crypto withdrawals are not supported. Minimum withdrawal $10. One cash-out per 7 days.
- **Hearsay (ClippingAlpha, 2026-10-24):** median $2/1K over 12 live campaigns, 0% open to join, MrBeast-affiliated, 37 payouts shown publicly.
- **Vendor (Vues blog, Aug 2026):** Vyro live campaigns "typically $1-2," which conflicts with ClippingAlpha's $2 median. Treat the rate as unresolved.
- **Fit (High):** the gaming campaigns (e.g. a MrBeast Gaming campaign, hearsay) match William's niche. The 5,000-views-per-post gate is the main barrier for a small channel.

### 2. Whop Content Rewards
- **Official whop.com page (logged out):** shows a community listing with a Join button. Campaign terms and fee were not readable without login.
- **Hearsay (ClippingAlpha, independent, 2026-10-24):** 682 live campaigns, 132 new a week, 10% creator fee on per-view pay, median $1.25/1K, $200 median cap per clip. Pays on day 10 after approval.
- **Hearsay, bot rules (ClippingAlpha):** a bot score past the campaign threshold pauses pay until a person checks. One appeal per flag.
- **Hearsay, fee conflict:** FindClout and an autoclip.dev article (both search snippets, 2026-08) say ~7%. ClippingAlpha says 10%. I have not resolved this from an official source.
- **Fit (Medium-High):** deepest board. Campaign rules vary, so each one needs a read-through before joining.

### 3. Promote.fun
- **Official site and terms (promote.fun, /terms-of-service):** not readable. Extraction returned no content on repeated tries.
- **Hearsay (ClippingAlpha, 2026-10-24):** escrow-funded, 77% of 17,659 submitted clips approved, median 4 days to pay after close (37 payouts), 0% open to join, median $1.63/1K.
- **Fit (Medium):** the payout record is the best among the platforms I checked, but the terms are unread.

### 4. Clipstake
- **Official FAQ (clipstake.com/faq, 2026-10-24):** brands set the RPM. Creators are paid per 1,000 views. Brands get audience analytics and botted-view filtering.
- **Hearsay (ClippingAlpha):** 8 live campaigns, 75% logo campaigns, USDC payout, $24K budget left.
- **Fit (Medium-Low):** small supply and a crypto payout. Both are weak for William.

### 5. Clipster
- **Official homepage (clipster.com):** brand-side. Brands set a budget and a rate per view. Clipper-side terms were not read.
- **Hearsay (ClippingAlpha):** 107 live campaigns, 54% music.
- **Fit (Low-Medium):** supply is weak for gaming.

### 6. Vues
- **Official ToS (vues.app/terms, 2026-10-24):**
  - §6: payouts "may include cryptocurrency (such as USDT), PayPal, or Zelle."
  - §9: "Permission for personal or non-commercial posting alone is not sufficient." Commercial permission must come from the rights holder.
  - §3: one account per person, no account creation by automated means.
  - §12: bans bots, scripts, click farms, view bots, and automation used to inflate views.
  - §18: arbitration. §19: Delaware law.
- **Vendor claims (vues.app/blog/best-clipping-platforms-2026, Aug 2026):** "best clipping platform" in 2026, $3M paid, 301,000+ approved clips. Self-published ranking, so treat as vendor claims only.
- **Campaign roster (my read of vues.app/campaigns, earlier in this run):** mostly gambling, crypto, and prediction-market campaigns. These conflict with William's rules.
- **Fit (Low):** the terms and roster both conflict with his rules. Vues would need a campaign-by-campaign screen before any use.

### 7. JoinBrands
- **Official creator page (2026-10-24):** flat fee per post: $100+ per social post, $60+ per video, $15+ per image. Paid via PayPal. Vendor claims $10M paid.
- **Fit (Low-Medium):** a flat per-post model is different from the CPM model in this project. Terms and platform list were not verified.

### 8-10. Clipping.net, Clipping.io, Reach.cat
- **Clipping.net:** the DuckDuckGo snippet says it runs pay-per-verified-view campaigns. Its campaign and brand pages returned 429 through fetch. The browser read worked, but I did not record the terms in this run. **Unverified.**
- **Clipping.io:** homepage was fetched in an earlier step, but the rates and terms were not recorded in this run. The only figure is the Vues vendor claim ($1-3 by campaign). **Unverified.**
- **Reach.cat:** fetch returned only a title (JavaScript page). Browser read worked, but the terms were not recorded in this run. **Unverified.**

## Biggest red flags

1. **Vues:** ToS allows crypto payouts. Its roster is mainly gambling and crypto. It requires commercial rights and bans automation that inflates views.
2. **Whop:** fee is 7% or 10% depending on the source. Bot-score pauses hold pay. Campaign terms are hidden without login.
3. **Vyro:** the 5,000-views-per-post gate means most posts from a small channel earn nothing.
4. **Clipstake:** crypto payout (hearsay). Small supply.
5. **Vendor rankings** (Vues, Ssemble): self-published, so they are not neutral sources.
