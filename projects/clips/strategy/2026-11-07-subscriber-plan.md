## 2. Tactics, ranked
Ranking weighs expected upside, effort, rules risk and evidence strength. All effect sizes are **[E]** in subscribers per 1,000 views (the measured ceiling today is about 0.65 per 1,000 [M, from 3/4,642]); confidence is low for every one. "Measure" always means `subscribersGained` per video (Analytics API: `ids=channel==MINE`, `dimensions=video`, metrics `views, estimatedMinutesWatched, averageViewDuration, subscribersGained, subscribersLost`, filtered to the clip IDs, read at 72 h and 7 d; per-video gains count subscribes from that video's watch surface, so compare against the channel-level total too).

1. **Fix measurement (do first).** Retry traffic-source and subscribed-status queries; re-pull all 16+ videos on 11/10; William supplies Studio screenshots for viewed-versus-swiped, channel-page views and subscriber count. Effect: none directly, but nothing below can be judged without it. Effort 1-2 h. Risk none.
2. **Series framing for every priority story.** Title prefix "Update n" (keep "Per HCSO:" style attribution), a playlist per story, and the Studio related-video link on each Short pointing at the previous or next clip (verify the field exists for Shorts). Effect +0.3 to 1.5 per 1,000 (E; the Citibank cluster is the natural first read). Effort low, the follow-up pipeline already creates sequences. Risk none beyond YouTube's repetitive-content rule, which allows "a series ... in which each video has a distinct storyline" [S]. Measure: subs per 1,000 views, series clips versus singles.
3. **On-screen follow promise in the last 2-3 s**: "Following this story. Follow for updates." Truthful, one line, no "sub for sub", no engagement bait. Effect +0.2 to 1.0 per 1,000 (E). Effort low if the Editor can add a text overlay in ffmpeg. Risk: may cost a little completion on a 25-37 s clip; guardrail below. Measure: Test 1.
4. **Channel page setup**: banner and About text that states the promise ("Official-source breaking-news clips; every clip names its source"), trailer or featured video (use the best long-form or Citibank summary), featured sections by story, playlists. Effect +0.1 to 0.5 per 1,000 (E), limited by how few reach the page. Effort 1 h, William's account. Risk none; the reviewer reads this section [S]. Measure: channel-page views and the before-versus-after subscribe rate (confounded; no-regret anyway).
5. **Long-form story recap (3-6 min)** from an official-source cluster, with a timeline and sourced captions, linked from the Shorts. This serves both subs and the long-form watch-hour leg. Effect (E): 0 to 10 subs per recap at a few hundred to 1,500 views; 0.5 to 5 watch hours per recap. Effort high (manual edit; the Volusia supercut today is the first). Risk: reused-content policy requires substantive edit [S]; use official sources only, no TV-network footage. Measure: Test 3.
6. **Context frame (2-3 s opening card with "what is confirmed so far", factual and attributed).** Adds original value, which the reused-content policy asks for [S], and gives the channel a recognizable look. Effect on subs +0 to 0.5 per 1,000 (E); main value is policy defense and identity. Effort medium. Risk: a card in the first seconds may raise swipe-away; test it on swipe rate before adopting. Keep it varied per clip, not a fixed template, because the generic-content policy flags templated output [S].
7. **Channel-owner pinned comment** with the story promise and the playlist link. Effect +0 to 0.3 per 1,000 (E). Effort low, but posting a comment needs an extra API scope or William's hand. Risk none. Measure: add as a flag in the log; too weak to test alone.
8. **Pick a beat (decision for William):** for example official briefings in Florida, or aviation incidents (NTSB/FAA). Our best two clips are the aviation pair and the biggest cluster is Tampa/Florida, but that is n=2 stories, so it is a hypothesis. Effect +0 to 1.0 per 1,000 through repeat viewers (E). Cost: fewer eligible stories, so fewer posts. Risk none. Measure: compare after day 30.
9. **Decide what to do with the 5 non-news Shorts (decision for William).** Making them private gives the page one subject; leaving them keeps their views (crate 1,200, GTA 1,100, Claude Code 745 per the 11/7 log [M, approximate]). Do not change this during the 14-day tests, because they may be giving the news clips a channel-level lift (unproven). Revisit on day 14. Effect: identity only.
10. **Story-keyword titles for search and suggested traffic.** News events produce searches; a clear title ("Citibank shooting: security officer stopped gunman, per HCSO") can capture intent-driven viewers. Effect unknown (E: small, but these viewers are not feed drive-bys). Effort none, already how titles are written. Risk none. Measure: traffic-source share from search.

**Not recommended, plainly:** subscribe-for-subscribe, "comment X" prompts, giveaways, buying views or subs, and posting TV-network re-cuts to chase views. On that last one: Island Boys (NBC 6) is our top clip but a copyright strike on a one-week-old channel is the worst downside in this whole plan, and it does not appear to help subscribers either (0 so far where measured, but that clip is not in the pull). Content ID claims are invisible in public data, so check Studio first (the playbook already says so).

## 3. 14-day experiment plan (2026-11-08 to 2026-11-21)
Volume assumption [E]: 3-4 news Shorts a day, so about 45-55 Shorts and about 35,000 views at the recent average of 700 views per clip (recent measured clips ran 560-1,400). At 10 subs per arm you need about 16,700 views at 0.06% or 4,000 views at 0.25% [E, 10 divided by the rate]. So we can tell 0.06% from 0.25% but not subtler gaps. Primary metric for all tests: **subscribersGained per 1,000 views, summed within an arm**. Randomize by coin flip recorded before posting; keep gaming and AI clips off the news stream; do not change other variables.

**Day 0-2 (11/8-11/10), no arm:** items 1 and 4 above (measurement fix, channel page). Re-pull on 11/10 to cover the Citibank cluster and Island Boys.

**Test 1: end-card follow promise (tactic 3).** Alternate assignment within each story (coin flip for the first clip, then alternate) so heat is balanced. n at least 16 clips and 15,000 views per arm. Success: promise arm at least 2x the control rate with at least 8 subs in the promise arm. Guardrail: viewed percentage (Studio) must not fall more than 10 points. Fail: under 4 subs in both arms means "no detectable effect at this volume".

**Test 2: series framing versus standalone (tactic 2).** Every story with 3 or more clips gets playlist, "Update n" titles and related-video links. Compare subs per 1,000 views against clips from single-clip stories. Not randomized, so directional only (story heat is the confounder; note it in the log). Success: series clips at least 2x standalone with at least 8 subs. Citibank (5 clips) counts as the first data point.

**Test 3: long-form recap (tactic 5).** Publish 2 recaps in the window (the Volusia supercut counts as recap 0). Read views at 72 h, average view duration, minutes per view, subs per 1,000 views. Success: subs per 1,000 views at least 3x the Shorts arm, or at least 5 subs total. Also log watch minutes. Arithmetic target for the hours leg: 4,000 h is 240,000 min, so a 3-minute average view needs 80,000 views [E, 3 min assumed].

**Test 4 (days 8-14 only): opening context card (tactic 6).** Randomize on clips from the same source type; n at least 10 per arm. Primary metric is Studio swiped-away rate and average view duration (a subscriber count will be too small to read). Adopt only if swipe-away does not rise and viewed percentage does not fall more than 5 points; else stop.

**Columns to add to experiment-log.csv** (existing `subs_or_followers_gained`, `views_72h`, `views_7d`, `swiped_away_or_2s_drop` stay): `test_id, arm, story_key, series_part, endcard_text (Y/N), context_card (Y/N), related_link (Y/N), playlist, subs_gained_72h, subs_lost_72h, subs_per_1k_views, minutes_watched, avg_view_duration_s, viewed_pct_engaged, viewed_vs_swiped_studio, search_share, channel_page_views_72h, analytics_pull_date`. Log arm assignment at posting time, before any result. Note the 2-3 day lag: 72 h reads are really available at about day 5, so the last clips posted after 11/17 will not be readable by 11/21.

**Decision gates:** day 14 (11/22): if no arm shows 2x with at least 8 subs and the pooled rate is still under 0.1%, stop investing effort in subscriber tactics and report to William that the Shorts-feed path is not converting. Day 30: re-check the pooled rate at 25,000+ views.

## 4. Timeline to 500 and 1,000 subscribers
Formula: views needed = subscribers divided by conversion rate; days = views needed divided by views per day. Views per day [M]: 4,642 public views over about 4.6 days since 11/3 is about 1,000 a day, which is a directional average (n = 4 days, a channel-level lift that may fade).

Assumptions [E, all]:
| Scenario | Rate | Views per day | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pessimistic | 0.02% (1 per 5,000) | 300 | Matches our 0 subs so far (expected 0.9 subs on 4,642 views); attention fades; no tactic works |
| Realistic | 0.06% (0.6 per 1,000) | 1,000 | Rate at the ceiling of what we have measured, so already generous; views stay at the recent average |
| Optimistic | 0.25% (2.5 per 1,000) | 3,000 | Series, follow promise and recap all work and the channel compounds |

| Scenario | Views for 500 | Views for 1,000 | Days to 500 | Days to 1,000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pessimistic | 2,500,000 | 5,000,000 | 8,333 (about 23 y) | 16,667 (about 46 y) |
| Realistic | 833,333 | 1,666,667 | 833 (about 2.3 y) | 1,667 (about 4.6 y) |
| Optimistic | 200,000 | 400,000 | 67 (about 2.2 mo) | 133 (about 4.4 mo) |

Sensitivity (views needed for 500 / 1,000 subs): 0.1% is 500,000 / 1,000,000; 0.5% is 100,000 / 200,000; 1.0% is 50,000 / 100,000 [E, arithmetic only; I do not claim any channel achieves these rates].

Required rate to hit a date: for 1,000 subs in 90 days you need 1.1% at 1,000 views a day (90,000 views) or 0.37% at 3,000 a day; in 180 days, 0.56% or 0.185%. For 500 subs the rates halve. [E arithmetic]

**Second leg (the real gate) [S for thresholds, E for arithmetic]:** early tier needs 3M Shorts views in 90 days (33,333 a day) or 3,000 long-form hours (180,000 min, 60,000 views at 3 min average); full tier needs 10M in 90 days (111,111 a day) or 4,000 hours (80,000 views at 3 min). Our best clip is about 1,400 views and total long-form watch time is 0 hours before today's supercut. Even the optimistic 3,000 views a day gives about 270,000 Shorts views in 90 days, 9% of the early-tier requirement. So YPP is a long-form watch-hour project on any realistic path, and subscribers and hours must both be built.

**Third gate:** the YPP review looks at the channel as a whole, including most-viewed videos and the About section, and lists "short videos you compiled from other social media websites" and content copied without substantive modification as not allowed to monetize, even with permission [S, YouTube channel monetization policies, fetched 2026-11-07]. Our Shorts are lightly edited clips of others' footage. A rejection means a 30-day wait before reapplying [S]. This is why tactics 5 and 6 matter beyond subscribers.
