## What was built
- `pipeline/reframe_short.py` (new): `render` crops a 9:16 window out of the 4K frame per shot (pans, slow pushes, punch-ins from a plan list in the spec) and burns the same hook/caption ASS as the band path. `analyze` finds the source's shots, runs YuNet face detection (green-cap face = "david", other = "host") and drafts a plan a human corrects against frames. No mirror, speed/pitch change, noise, outside visuals or music.
- `pipeline/clip_job.py`: optional spec fields `layout` (band default | reframe) and `reframe` (plan). The band `format` call is the same as before. Also: `instagram_caption` optional (TikTok-only specs); `--approve`'s overlap test compares by campaign instead of by source file (a 4K and a 1080p copy of one episode are different files, so the old test would have missed overlaps between band and reframe specs); reframe cuts use crf 14 instead of 18. Prior version: `~/openclaw-sites/clip_job.py.pre-reframe`. `format_short.py` untouched.
- Dependencies outside the repo: `~/openclaw-sites/reframe-venv` (opencv-python-headless), `~/openclaw-sites/reframe-models/yunet.onnx`. Documented in `pipeline/README.md` ("REFRAME layout").
- Same in both arms: style 1 (font/colour), hook rules (3-8 words, no spoiled payoff), one caption line, `#unpacked` and `@hardscope` once each in the TikTok caption. Different by design: reframe text sits in the lower third (faces fill the top); band text is where it always was.
- The band specs render from the 1080p copy exactly as before; reframe specs from the 4K original.

- **No jitter or drift seen:** crops are static or linear moves, no per-frame tracking. Not checked: playback on a phone.
- **Band arm:** unchanged format; the sharp footage is a strip about a third of the screen height, as in all 19 earlier posts.
