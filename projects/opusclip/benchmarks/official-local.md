# Benchmark: official-source and local-news clippers (YouTube Shorts)

Pulled 2026-11-06 (~16:00-16:20 ET) with yt-dlp, metadata only (no downloads). Read-only research; nothing posted or submitted, no OpusClip credits used.
Scope: local TV stations' own Shorts plus court/legal outlets. All times America/New_York. All conclusions are **directional**.

**Sample:** the latest 24-30 Shorts per channel (the whole Shorts tab as returned by yt-dlp). For high-volume channels that is only 1-4 days of posts, so a 14-day posts/day figure could not be computed. Posts/day is over the sampled span. "Breakout" = a post with more than 3x the channel's own median views in the sample.

## Titles

- **Plain headline sentence, sentence case, no hashtags, no ALL-CAPS, no "BREAKING".** NBC 6, WPLG, KTLA, ABC7 Chicago, ABC7 NY and NBC10 all use 0 hashtags and 0-1 all-caps titles in the sample. WPLG had one "breaking" title; the others had none. Titles are written in the station's own voice with no credit to a source agency.
- **Official-source verbs lead the hits:** "NTSB: Aircraft 'was breaking up' before impact", "Learjet may have broken apart before crash" (same Palm Beach jet crash story on WPLG and KTLA), "Bodycam video shows...", "Video captures...", "DeSantis: 'This is close to an extinction-level event...'". Only about 10-20% of titles per station used police, bodycam, court or hearing wording (NBC 6 4/30, WPLG 5/25, KTLA 3/24, ABC7 Chicago 5/25, NBC10 2/25, ABC7 NY 0/25, WFLA 3/25).
- **Station branding in the title is rare.** Only FOX 5 Atlanta (stale) tagged every title with "| FOX 5 News", and NBC10 had 3 titles with a "| NBC10 Philadelphia" suffix. Court TV (stale) used hashtags in 21 of 25 titles.

## Pattern bullets (directional)

- **Cadence is high and steady for the big stations.** NBC 6, WPLG, KTLA, ABC7 Chicago and ABC7 NY post roughly 7-18 Shorts a day, with a median gap of 0.3-1.9 hours between posts. NBC10 is lighter and bursty (~3/day average, with whole days at zero). A smaller operation (WFLA) posts about one per weekday.
- **Timing is a business-day spread, with an afternoon bias.** The 10:00-18:00 window carries most posts: for NBC 6, KTLA and ABC7 NY, 10-18h holds 16 of 30, 16 of 24 and 15 of 25 posts. The top single hours are 12-15h for NBC 6, KTLA, ABC7 NY and ABC7 Chicago (also 9h), and 8-12h for WPLG. NBC10 differs, with an evening/late bump (21-22h) plus a midday peak. WFLA posts only 9-10h. Because the sample covers 1-4 days for most channels, treat the exact top hours as noise-prone; the stable signal is "all day, weekday daytime heavy".
- **Weekend behavior is unknown.** Every sampled span for the high-volume channels falls within Mon-Fri (the pull happened on Friday), so the weekday/weekend split could not be measured. Only NBC10 (8 posts on Sunday Nov 1) shows any weekend activity.
- **Breakout rate is about 8-20% of posts (>3x the channel median)** for the six active channels (WFLA had 0/25). The mean sits well above the median everywhere (e.g. WPLG median 920 vs mean 4.5K), so a few hits carry the channel. Hit multiples are large: WPLG's top clip was 46x its median, ABC7 Chicago's 89x. Hits are mostly breaking local or national stories (jet crash with an NTSB quote, train hitting a semi, missing-child case, election results), plus one or two novelty clips (zoo tigers with a pumpkin).
- **Views per post are low relative to subs for most stations.** Medians run roughly 0.08-1.7% of subscribers per Short (WPLG 920 views on 1.12M subs = 0.08%; KTLA 13.5K on 1.52M = 0.9%; NBC 6 10.5K on 632K = 1.7%; NBC10 2.5K on 229K = 1.1%). The newest clips (posted in the last few hours) are under-counted, so the newest 3-6 posts per channel should be ignored when judging performance.
- **Length is short for most.** Median 27-28s (WPLG, ABC7 Chicago), 40-52s (NBC 6, KTLA), 60-67s (ABC7 NY, NBC10); WFLA's clips run 97-144s. The top clips by views were usually 14-60s, except ABC7 NY's 98s and 122s Brooklyn and Queens crime stories, which landed in its top three.
- **Speed to post is unknown.** yt-dlp gives the upload time but not the time of the underlying press conference or briefing. One data point: the NTSB Palm Beach jet crash briefing clip went up on WPLG at Mon 22:57 ET and drew 28K views (30x its median); KTLA posted a related clip Wed 16:39 with 163K views (12x its median). I cannot say how long after the briefing either went up. Median gaps of 0.3-1.9h between posts suggest same-day turnaround is normal, but that is inferred, not measured.

## Caveats

- Views are a weak proxy for success: no impressions, retention, subscribers gained or revenue are visible, and view counts depend on post age.
- n is small (24-30 Shorts per channel, 1-8 days for the busy channels). No weekend data for 5 of 7 channels. One channel was sampled across 39 days, the others across 1-8 days, so figures are not comparable on time span.
- yt-dlp reported 278 subs for WFLA News, which looks implausible for a TV station and may belong to a secondary handle. Treat it as unverified.
- These are stations' own Shorts, not third-party re-cutters. I did not find an active independent clipper channel dedicated to press conference, NTSB/FAA/NWS briefing, hearing or 911 re-cuts with clear traction (Court TV is the closest and is stale on Shorts; "Police TV Shorts" and "Police Videos" appeared in search but are compilation-style and were not analyzed). Findings reflect how institutional newsrooms post, which may not match a solo clipper's cadence.
- Shorts over 60s are counted as Shorts only because they appear in the channel's Shorts tab.
- Scripts used (outside any data dir): `/tmp/clip_scripts/pull.sh`, `analyze.py`, `extra.py`; raw metadata in `/tmp/clip_data/*.jsonl`.
