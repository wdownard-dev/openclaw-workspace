# Reuse permissions research: gaming creators (read-only, 2026-11-06)

Research, not legal advice. No one was contacted. Every claim carries a source URL; "Unknown" means no policy was found.
Rating key: Green = explicit permission/CC/open clip policy. Amber = tolerated in practice, no explicit policy. Red = known to claim/strike or prohibits. Unknown = nothing found.

## 1. YouTube monetization policy: reused content (applies to every creator below)

Source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392 (fetched 2026-11-06; page notes July 15, 2025 rename of "repetitious" to "inauthentic content" and "There is no change to our reused content policy which reviews content like commentary, clips, compilations, and reaction videos.")

Definition (verbatim): "Reused content refers to channels that repurpose content that's already on YouTube or another online source without adding significant original commentary, substantive modifications, or educational or entertainment value."

Scope (verbatim): "Our reused content policy applies to your channel as a whole. If you have videos that violate our guidelines, or if we cannot clearly tell that you made the content, monetization may be removed from your entire channel."

Allowed (verbatim, selected): "we allow reused content if viewers can tell that there's a meaningful difference between the original video and your video." Examples: "Using clips for a critical review"; "Reaction videos where you comment on the original video"; "Edited footage from other creators where you add a storyline and commentary"; "Edited footage with audio and visual effects on top of the video's reused content that demonstrates substantive editing and shows it's unique to your channel".

Not allowed (verbatim, selected): "Taking someone else's content, making minimal changes, and calling it your own original work would be a violation of this guideline." Also: "This policy applies even if you have permission from the original creator. Reused content is separate from YouTube's Copyright enforcement, which means it's not based on copyright, permission, or fair use." Examples of violations: "Clips of moments from your favorite show edited together with little or no narrative"; "Short videos you compiled from other social media websites"; "Promotion of other people's content (even if you have permission)"; "Content downloaded or copied from another online source without any substantive modifications".

Reviewer behavior (verbatim): reviewers "may focus on your channel's: Main theme, Most viewed videos, Newest videos, Biggest proportion of watch time, Video metadata (including titles, thumbnails, and descriptions), Channel's 'About' section."

Implication: even a creator's explicit permission does NOT protect monetization. Commentary-light re-cuts of another person's video are the example YouTube lists as non-monetizable. "Source: @handle" credit does not change that. The risk is channel-wide, not per video.

Content ID (how claims happen, separate from the above): https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2797370. "When a video is uploaded to YouTube, it's automatically scanned by Content ID. If Content ID finds a match, the matching video will get a Content ID claim." Outcomes: block, monetize (ads against the video, sometimes sharing revenue with the uploader), or track.

## 2. Publisher policies (CoD / GTA)

### 2.1 YouTube Shorts monetization (added, relevant to both channels)
Source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220
- "Ineligible Shorts views ... Non-original Shorts, such as unedited clips from others' movies or TV shows, reuploading other creators' content from YouTube or other platform, or compilations with no original content added". Those views are not counted for Shorts ad revenue.
- "Shorts over one minute in length that contain claimed content are blocked and not eligible for monetization."
- "Starting February 1, 2027, to be eligible to earn a share of Shorts ad revenue from the Creator Pool and YouTube Premium revenue for each month, you will need to have at least 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days." (as written on the page fetched 2026-11-06)

### 2.2 Credit and disclaimers do not equal fair use or permission
Source: https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9783148
- "Giving credit to the owner of a copyrighted work won't by itself turn a non-transformative copy of their material into fair use. Phrases like 'all rights go to the author' and 'I do not own' don't automatically mean you're making fair use of that material. They also don't mean you have the copyright owner's permission."
- "Source: @handle" credit therefore protects nothing by itself. Fair use is decided case by case by courts, per the same page.

## 5. Key caveats
1. YouTube's reused-content rule is independent of permission and credit. A creator's OK (including xQc's) does not protect channel monetization, and the policy applies to the whole channel. Commentary-light re-cuts are the case YouTube lists as non-monetizable. Reviewers look at titles, most-viewed videos and the About section.
2. Gameplay footage has a second rights holder. Rockstar's terms reserve rights in gameplay clips and livestreams; the dedicated Rockstar policy page and any Activision creator policy could not be read here, so GTA 6 and MW4 clips need a manual check of those pages.
3. Absence of a policy is not permission. For nine of thirteen creators only Unknown or Amber is supportable; asking for written permission (routes above) is the only way to move any of them to Green, and even then caveat 1 applies.
