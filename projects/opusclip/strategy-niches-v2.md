- **Correction [judgment]:** aim at **18 to 29, male-leaning, as the core** (TikTok skews there, and drama and gaming clips pull it). **30 to 40 is a secondary band** that comes through YouTube (91% use it), mostly for news and AI-money. Teens are fine to reach but are not a target: low ad value, and content aimed at kids carries extra YouTube limits. Which age band prefers which genre has no source I could verify, so treat that as my read.
- **What stops the scroll.** Evidence is thin. **[own]** mw4-08 (n=1) put a whole idea in 6 words and worked on mute; the 10/30 diagnosis found spoiler hooks and slow openers hurt. **[V, observational, not a test]** The biggest recent results I saw today (Asmongold TV "I got BETRAYED.." 720K views in 8 h; MrBeast "Beat Haaland, Win $100,000" 60M in 3 days) all open on a named person in a conflict or a number at stake. Working hook list **[judgment]**: (1) the conflict line first, "X confronts Y"; (2) a stake or number ("$1,000/hour"); (3) shock reaction plus a caption saying what just happened; (4) an outcome that breaks the viewer's expectation; (5) a clutch or relatable moment, no setup. Emotions: outrage, fear and curiosity, schadenfreude, awe. In 2 seconds the frame needs a face, a change, and a caption that states the stakes.

## 4. How a breaking-news lane could realistically work
- **Speed [judgment]:** the useful window is roughly hour 2 to hour 48 after a source posts. Post within 24 h of the source, or drop it.
- **Finding stories [V, all worked today]:** Google Trends RSS (`trends.google.com/trending/rss?geo=US`), the TMZ and YouTube "this week" search, and `scout.py` on the aviation and creator channels in section 3. Pick stories only when an official or primary source exists.
- **How we stay transformative:** cut to one decisive 30-45 s moment from a primary source (a briefing, a press conference, a court stream, the creator's own words), add an on-screen "what we know / what is not confirmed" line and a date stamp, credit the source, and write original captions. Add a weekly 8-10 min "week in X" with our own voice-over; that is the original work YouTube's reused-content policy looks for.
- **Cadence:** at most 3 to 4 news clips per week, because of credits and review time.
- **Own channel or lane?** A lane inside wrdclipz, with a "Breaking" tag and a playlist. Spin it out only if, after at least 10 news clips, its median 72 h views are at least 2x the lead lane, and a repeatable primary-source pipeline exists.

## 5. One identity
A mixed "hot clips" channel can get Shorts views, but the subscribe promise and the weekly compilation need a theme, and YouTube's reviewers look at the channel's main theme **[V, reused-content policy]**. So: **lead = creator and streamer moments**; **tests = news lane, AI lane**. If news clearly wins the 7-day test, it gets its own channel rather than diluting this one.

## 7. The 9.7 auto-post rule for breaking news
- **No auto-post for the news lane.** The score predicts nothing about accuracy. Keep 9.7 as the auto lane for lead and AI clips, with the safety rules from the first report (source in `channels.txt`, no slurs, no minors, no claims).
- **News fast lane:** a human sign-off in about 60 to 90 minutes, from a score floor of 9.0. A clip skips if it is not posted within 48 h of the source.
- **Accuracy rules (all must pass):** (1) every on-screen claim traces to the cut source or an official statement; (2) use "NTSB says", "police say", "reported", "alleged"; never "confirmed" or a cause unless an official said it; (3) no victim names, photos, minors, cockpit-distress audio or graphic footage; (4) no guilt implied about anyone not charged; no health, overdose or relationship rumors; (5) no election or partisan content; (6) the caption has the source and its date; (7) if the source issues a correction, delete or fix our clip within an hour; (8) skip sources whose own title is speculation ("What really happened", "The plane did NOT crash").
- **Credit guardrail:** news sources of 20 minutes or less only; about 70 credits per week covers everything.
