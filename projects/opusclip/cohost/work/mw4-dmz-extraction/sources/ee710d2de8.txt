Part 2: Tour of Duty – Forward Operating Base (FOB) Full Intel Guide
A tour of the FOB stations, with information on 3D Printer crafting, Bounties, Weapon acquisition, Stashes and Loadouts, Active Duty Operators, DMZ Operator Prestiging, Permanent Prestige Traits, and DMZ Player Progression to Level 70. You’re reading this now!
Welcome to the Forward Operating Base.
Forward Operating Base (FOB): Overview
Available Immediately / Accessed from the DMZ Main Menu 
The Forward Operating Base (FOB) is your home between DMZ deployments. This navigable encampment allows you to offload your loot, gear up, receive your next Orders, before you head back into Hajin. The FOB is also home to DMZ’s permanent progression, including customizing your Active Duty Operators.
As you conduct further deployments into the Exclusion Zone, the FOB unlocks and upgrades into a fully established base.
Throughout the FOB, there are various stations, and they can be critical to your survival when deployed in the Exclusion Zone. Some stations are unlocked through playing DMZ and can then be upgraded using the 3D Printer station. These stations allow you to track down dangerous Lieutenants, build out your perfect weapon, swap out your Active Duty Operator, craft powerful gear, and more. 
Here are the Stations that your FOB is comprised of, starting with those you should access first:
Operators (Active Duty):
 Manage your available Operators, check their status, upgrade their Traits, and select one ready for deployment.
DMZ Orders:
 DMZ Orders are your guiding Mission Directives in DMZ. These assist with getting you onboarded, telling the story of DMZ and challenging you to master the exclusion zone.
Stash/Loadout:
 The Stash allows you to store a variety of loot types from Weapons to Gear to 3D Printer Ingredients and more! Every time you extract, make sure to offload items you'll need in future deployments. Equip weapons and gear in your Loadout, ready for the next infil.
Firing Range: 
Test your weapons (especially your optics) and sharpen your aim in this wooded grove, with targets positioned from close to more than 100 meters away.
Survival Kits:
 These are themed packs of loot items that can be opened and then used to gear up or used to craft.
Bounty Leaderboard:
 Track the most successful PvP bounty hunters and killers across the Exclusion Zone.
Continue your successful deployments across Hajin, and you’re soon able to expand your FOB, and unlock additional stations:
3D Printer (Unlocks at DMZ Player Level 2): 
This uses a mix of Printer Resources and 3D Printer Ingredients to craft a variety of gear. The 3D Printer is also used to upgrade various stations in the FOB provided you have the required loot items.
Boss Board (Unlocks after you complete your second DMZ Order):
 Purchase intel on Lieutenants, then locate and prepare to hunt down Hajin’s most powerful adversaries. Additional information is also available for Commanders.
Vendor (Unlocks at DMZ Player Level 5):
 The Vendor has a variety of weapons and other items for sale using DMZ Cash. The vendor’s wares rotate day to day so get this gear while it’s available!
Gunsmith (Unlocks at DMZ Player Level 10):
 Use in-game DMZ Cash to purchase attachments for looted weapons and to build brand new weapons from scratch. The attachments you can build are related to the level of the weapon and the shared attachments unlocked for that weapon. To increase the weapons you can build in Gunsmith you need to find and extract Weapon Manuals.
Dog Tag Case (Unlocks at DMZ Player Level 20):
 Display Dog Tags collected from defeated Operators and favorite the ones you don’t want to lose.

The last stations are immediately available, and detail your full DMZ Player Progression, as well as confirming your deployment into Hajin:
DMZ Progression: 
DMZ has its own dedicated Progression Track which awards key Printer Recipes, Unlocks FOB stations, and awards various other content all the way up to level 70!
Deploy: 
A threshold of razor wire leading to a heavy-lift military transport helicopter where you’ll begin each DMZ deployment.
Operators Station: Active Duty
Available Immediately / Accessed within the FOB, or the left side blade menu.
The 
Operators Station
 is where you can view, manage, and 
select your Active Duty Operators
. In DMZ, Operators are generally a cosmetic preference, individual characters who grow stronger as you survive deployments and award them Traits. You earn 
DMZ Player Level XP
 with them.
Each Operator comes with a set of Dog Tags. As an Active Duty Operator progresses, the quality of these Dog Tags improves, and Trait Points are awarded. Trait Points can be spent across three Trait Trees, allowing you to specialize in different playstyles. Ultimately, your Active Duty Operator can earn permanent Prestige Traits that are tied to each individual Operator. 
Recruiting an Operator
Firstly, recruit an Operator to an open slot. The Operator you pick for a slot is locked to that slot until they are dismissed by you in favor of a replacement. 
Example: If you select Reece as an Operator, you cannot change to another Operator. You also cannot choose Reece again in another Active Duty slot.
Operator Skins: You can change Reece’s Operator Skin in this menu.
You begin with two available Active Duty Operator slots and can unlock another slot by progressing your DMZ level.
Players with the
 Modern Warfare 4 Vault Edition
 have access to an additional Active Duty slot and start with three instead of two.
Understanding the Active Duty Basics
Keep these Active Duty basics in mind:
Manage multiple Operators:
 Maintain multiple Active Duty Operators, giving you different builds to choose from.
Earn Trait Points:
 Successfully earn DMZ XP with an Operator to upgrade their Dog Tags, receiving a Trait Point each time you level up your Dog Tags. Traits are used to improve that specific Operator in various ways.
Choose your Traits:
 Spend Trait Points across three different playstyle Trait Trees; Warfighter, Scout, and Survivalist.
Create specialized Operators:
 A single Operator can earn up to 18 Trait Points.
Use your Active Duty slots and Trait selections to build Operators around different playstyles.
MIA Operators
If an Operator is eliminated during a deployment, they become 
MIA (Missing in Action)
 and cannot deploy again until they are recovered.
Recover an MIA Operator:
 Spend 
DMZ Cash
 to bring an MIA Operator back while preserving all of their Traits. Access the Operators station and pay DMZ Cash from your in-game Bank to rescue them.
Progression and Cost:
 The cost to recover an Operator is determined by their Trait Points. The more Traits, the more expensive their return.
Reequip:
 Recovered Operators return 
without their gear
, so equip a new Loadout before deploying again.
Dismiss and start over:
 Instead of recovery, you can dismiss an MIA Operator and start fresh with a new Operator.
Operator Dog Tag
Each Operator has a Dog Tag available to inspect in your Loadout, that represents the progression of the Operator wearing it. It can be removed if the Operator dies during a deployment and is recovered if an MIA Operator is brought back to Active Duty. The higher the quality (or Level) of the Dog Tag, the higher the progression of the Operator. This Dog Tag also shows the Bounty status of the Operator, and the enemy Operator kill count.
As you progress and earn XP within DMZ, your Dog Tag’s Level progresses, allowing you to unlock Traits:
Progression (18 Levels):
Steel (I, II, III)
Bronze (I, II, III)
Silver (I, II, III)
Gold (I, II, III)
Damascus (I, II, III)
Onyx (I, II, III)
The Level of your Dog Tag is worth noting, as rival Operators can defeat you in combat, and loot your belongings, including your Dog Tag, to display in their Dog Tag Case (see later in this Blog).
Earn XP to increase your Dog Tag Level, receiving a Trait Point every time you reach a new Dog Tag Level. This is the key to accessing Traits.
An Operator’s level (indicated by their Dog Tag quality) and a player’s overall DMZ level are independent. An Operator is considered fully leveled up when the Onyx III is reached, and 18 Trait Points have been earned. At this point, you can Prestige the Operator (see below).
The Three Trait Trees
These are accessed from the quick navigation left-blade menu for your currently equipped Operator and is also accessible via the Operator menu. Any Trait Tree Points you earn after successfully earning a Dog Tag level up after an exfil as that Operator are accessible here. You can have multiple Operators active (as many as you have slots) with each individually earning Trait Points. Spend Trait Points to acquire Traits from one of three Trees:
Warfighter
Scout
Survivalist
There are 66 Traits available in DMZ, spread equally across the three Trees. Not all of them can be accessed by your Operator, as you can only have a maximum of 18 Trait Points before you fully level up your Operator and reach Onyx III Dog Tag status. Here’s how this works:
Every successful exfil awards you DMZ XP, and the level of your Dog Tag is affected by this. Acquire enough and you level up your Dog Tag, earning one Trait Point each time. If you’ve done particularly well during a deployment, you may level up your Dog Tag more than once.
When you’ve acquired your first Trait Point, study the three Trait Trees closely. Each Tree has a total of 22 different Traits you can access.
Each Trait Tree consists of the following:
Six Major Traits (always visible, arranged in groups of two aside from the initial Trait pick).
16 Minor Traits (randomized, including the random Trait you obtain when you first unlock each Tree. Minor Traits are only visible if you’ve unlocked the previous Main Trait below the Minor group and arranged in groups of three). 
Plan ahead to pick your preferred Main Trait, and then the available Minor Traits that complement your overall goals or playstyle along the way. Choose to unlock more Traits from an existing Trait Tree, or Traits from a different tree.
Spend your first Trait point on the initial (random) Minor Trait for that tree. The remaining Traits are in branches of two (Major) and three (Minor). 
Unlock one of the Traits from a previous branch to reach the next branch. Then choose a single Trait from that branch.
Continue until you’ve either completed unlocking a Tree (unlocking a maximum of nine Traits, one per branch) or unlocked a variety of branches from multiple Trees, as shown in the adjacent Trait menu screenshot.
Once you pick a Trait, it is locked in and can’t be substituted.
Therefore, with 18 Trait Points before you maximize your Operator level (and no more Trait Points are awarded), you can elect to max out two of the three Trait Trees or open all three to varying degrees of completion.
Remember: The more Traits you have, the more expensive your Operator is to rescue if they are downed during a deployment.
Example Traits
There’s a great deal of variety of Traits to uncover as you progress your Operator, especially as these can (eventually) become permanently unlocked if you decide to Prestige in DMZ. Here are examples of the six Main (visible) Traits for each of the three Trait Trees, and one initially-awarded random Minor Trait that became available when our Operator first accessed each of the three Trait Trees:
Main Warfighter Traits
Adrenaline Burst:
 Killing an enemy resets tactical sprint.
Equipment Professional: 
Slowly recharges equipped Lethals and Tacticals after use.
Combat Resistance: 
Reduces damage from explosives and the effects of enemy Tacticals.
Viper Sense:
 Conceals you from thermal optics and AI targeting systems.
Quick Fix: 
Reduces health regeneration delay when an enemy operator or hostile is killed.
Possum: 
Allows a Self Revive into Last Stand from death, restoring health to maximum, once per match.
Adaptive Restock: 
Enemy hostile drop one additional item.
Main Scout Traits
Investor: 
Slightly increases cash amount extracted.
Hostile Looter: 
Enemy hostiles sometimes drop an Uncommon printer ingredient, with a very small chance for a Rare printer ingredient.
Container Looter: 
Chance to get extra piece of loot in containers.
Thermal Padding:
 Hides from UAVs and downgrades Advanced UAVs to UAV detection.
UAV Upgrade:
 Increases the range and duration of UAVs. Also upgrades them to Advanced UAVs.
Overwatch: 
UAV towers you activate never expire.
Field Crafting:
 Allows crafting items directly out of Common printer resources while deployed.
Main Survivalist Traits
Air Superiority:
 Extends the duration of UAV towers and SAM sites.
Faster Revive:
 Increases Revive speeds.
Consumable Duration: 
Increases the duration of consumables.
Impact Smokes: 
Releases smoke when downed.
Free Revive: 
Automatically repairs your first Wound.
Numb the Pain:
 Applies Painkillers and Smelling Salts effects on revives.
Die Hard: 
Approaching an exfil while in Last Stand calls the exfil automatically. Also grants infinite bleed out time while in Last Stand.
Operators Station: DMZ Operator Prestige
Available after an Active Duty Operator earns 18 Trait Points / Accessed at the Operators station or Traits side menu
Once an Active Duty Operator earns 18 Trait Points, you can Prestige the Operator as long as they aren’t MIA and require rescuing. DMZ Operator Prestige is separate from the standard Level 1–55 Player Progression, the 1-70 DMZ Player Progression and other MW4 Prestige systems. It is tied to a specific Operator.
DMZ Operator Prestige is optional, the requirements to Prestige can be a challenge, but the reward is worth it: With each Operator Prestige,
 a special random Prestige Trait is equipped permanently to that Operator
. Operators can earn up to three Prestige Traits.
Here are the requirements for your Operator to Prestige for the first time:
Requirement 1: Maximum Operator Level:
 You must have earned a total of 18 Trait Points with the Operator. The Trait Points don’t need to be spent, though it is recommended you do so.
Requirement 2: Equip Gear of Specific Rarity: 
Acquire and equip high Rarity Loadout equipment to the Operator’s Loadout and Backpack. For the first Prestige, you need:
Two Weapons (Rare Rarity or higher, each with three Attachment slots)
Plate Carrier (Rare Rarity or higher)
Backpack (Rare Rarity or higher)
Printer Ingredients (4 total, Rare Rarity or higher)
Remember to remove any items you don’t want to sacrifice from your Backpack or Loadout too; these are lost when you Prestige.
Requirement 3: Required Cash:
 You must have sufficient DMZ Cash funds, deducted from your in-game DMZ Cash Bank.
Meet all three requirements, and once you place your Gear in your Loadout and select Prestige in DMZ, all the required Gear is removed, the payment of in-game Funds occurs, and your Operator’s Traits are removed and reset.
Then, a random, 
Permanent Prestige Trait
 is granted to your Operator.
Permanent Prestige Operator Traits
Here’s what you need to know about Permanent Prestige Traits:
The Operator (and only that Active Duty Operator) gains the Prestige Trait.
This is randomly selected from the available Trait pool.
Permanent Prestige Traits are just that: They remain tied to the Operator, even if you dismiss them, assign them to a different Active Duty slot, or change their Operator skin.
At this point, you can continue developing your Operator, working towards a specialized soldier, supremely skilled in specific acts of modern warfare.
DMZ Prestige: Levels 2 and 3
After Prestiging, you earn Trait Tree Points over again as before. If your Active Duty Operator reaches maximum level again (18 Trait Tree Points) you can Prestige for a second time.
 The requirements increase depending on the Operator Prestige level:
DMZ First Prestige: 
Two Weapons with three Attachment Slots.
One Plate Carrier (Rare Rarity)
One Backpack (Rare Rarity)
Four 3D Printer Ingredients (Rare Rarity)
200,000 DMZ Cash
DMZ Second Prestige:
Two Weapons with four Attachment Slots.
One Plate Carrier (Epic Rarity)
One Backpack (Epic Rarity)
Four 3D Printer Ingredients (Epic Rarity)
300,000 DMZ Cash
DMZ Third Prestige (and every Prestige thereafter):
Two Weapons with four Attachment Slots.
One Plate Carrier (Legendary Rarity)
One Backpack (Legendary Rarity)
Four 3D Printer Ingredients (Legendary Rarity)
500,000 DMZ Cash
Each time you Prestige, you receive an additional, random Permanent Prestige Trait.
Your Operator can have a maximum of three Permanent Prestige Traits.
Beyond Prestige Level 3
Once you’ve received your third Permanent Prestige Trait, the third set of prestige requirements remain in place for every subsequent Prestige.
You can continue to Prestige almost indefinitely. However, instead of receiving a new Prestige Trait, the following occurs at Level 4 (or higher):
You receive another Permanent Trait, different from the three you already have.
Discard
 one of your three existing Permanent Traits, and replace it with the one you received.
Or, keep all three of your current Permanent Traits and discard the newly offered one.
Continue to Prestige your Operator, begin to hone your Trait talents once again, and build a roster of elite, tier one specialists, each honed to your favorite playstyles, and ready to take on Hajin’s toughest deployments.
[CLASSIFIED] Additional Prestige Rewards
Aside from Permanent Traits tied to an Operator, Prestiging multiple Operators earns additional Calling Cards and [CLASSIFIED] rewards.
DMZ Orders Station
Available Immediately / Accessed within the FOB / Orders Available in the Top Corner of your FOB HUD.
Visit the 
DMZ Orders station
 to view your current objectives— organized in Pages — and the rewards available for completing them. DMZ Orders are your guiding mission directives throughout DMZ, training you, helping you upgrade your Operator and FOB, deliver narrative intel, and offer challenges. Learn these fundamentals to help you succeed in future deployments.
Completing DMZ Orders will earn you resources, loot and cash to complete objectives. Rewards will get you geared up for future deployments as you upgrade your FOB. Orders are always available and are a great way to progress through DMZ.
Stash/Loadout Station
Available Immediately / Accessed within the FOB, or the left side blade menu.
The Stash/Loadout station is separated into two areas:
Stash 
allows you to offload loot after a successful deployment, where it is shared by all your Active Duty Operators. It also allows you to gear up before entering back into Hajin. Items in the Stash remain in the FOB.
Backpack:
 Items and equipment, as well as spaces for weapons and Secure Slots once you upgrade your Backpack, are stored here. These are accessible during deployment, and anything not placed in a Secure Slot is lost if you fail to Exfil.
The Loadout
 is the equipment your Operator carries and actively uses in a deployment and returns with after a successful exfil. 
Keep these Stash and Loadout basics in mind:
Build your Loadout:
 Select weapons and items from your Stash to equip your Operator before deploying.
Store items and weapons:
 Remove any unwanted Loadout items and place them in the Stash. Any 3D printed items are also stored in the Stash. Your Stash initially holds up to 
40 items
 and 
12 weapons
, with weapons counted separately from general items. In DMZ you can save Weapons, Gear, Consumables, 3D Printer Ingredients, Keys, and even valuables you don’t want to sell.
Manage your inventory:
 Successful deployments can quickly fill your Backpack, so it’s wise to offload your loot into the Stash. Review what you want to keep, sell, discard (in the rare event you run out of Stash room and your Bank is capped at maximum saved funds), or use for crafting. The Stash always shows items by Type, then Rarity. There are options to sort by Rarity and then Type, or you can Merge loot stacks to keep your Stash organized.
Crafting Resources:
 There are four 
types of Resources
 — Synthetics, Electronics, Chemicals, and Metals — that you automatically break down during looting. The totals of these materials are shown in the bottom left corner of the Stash Menu, and don’t take up Stash slots. 
Use them at the 3D Printer station
.
3D Printer Ingredients:
 Other items collected during a deployment that don’t automatically break down are known as 3D Printer Ingredients and are stored in your Backpack during a deployment. After a successful exfil, move these to your Stash to free up Backpack space, and use the 3D Printer Ingredients to craft new gear, and to upgrade stations in the FOB in the 3D Printer Upgrade menu. Use them wisely.
TIP:
 All DMZ Cash, Resources, 3D Printer Ingredients, and other content you acquire in DMZ, is shared by all your Active Duty Operators. Think about gathering loot using an Operator skilled in finding valuable items thanks to their Traits and bring these back for other Operators you are utilizing.
Initial Deployment: Basic Pack and Loadout
Basic Pack
Without a proper Backpack equipped, you begin with the Basic Pack, with eight available slots. Each item has a defined stack size, meaning you can carry multiples of smaller items in a single slot. All backpacks include a specific pouch for Resources to collect; this way you don’t have to manage your Chemicals, Electronics, Metals, or Synthetics (all of which are automatically broken down from items you loot during a deployment and used along with 3D Printer Ingredients to craft items at the 3D Printer). Additional types of Backpacks add to the number and type of item slots. 
Loadout
This details the weapons and gear you have immediate access to and are carrying with you into Hajin. See your DMZ Loadout in the center image, above.
DMZ Cash:
 This is always empty prior to deployment as it shows the available funds found during a deployment. These are added to your Bank after a successful exfil.
Armor:
 This is always empty prior to deployment. Once you infil, your Armor Slot is automatically filled with plates. The only way to bring in more Armor Plates is by choosing Traits that expand the Armor Slot capacity, and/or by bringing in the Armor Box Field Upgrade.
Dog Tag:
 Your Dog Tag, Bounty, and Operator Kills ratings.
Headgear:
 Items worn on the head, such as Gas Masks or NVGs.
Self-Revive:
 A specific slot for Self-Revive Kits.
Weapons Slot 1:
 Your main Primary or Secondary Weapon.
Plate Carrier:
 Your equipped Armor Vest.
Weapons Slot 2:
 Your additional Primary or Secondary Weapon.
Tactical, Lethal, Backpack, Field Upgrade, Fire Support (Killstreak):
 Instantly accessible equipment goes here. Store extra in your Backpack.
Mission Item:
 This is always empty prior to deployment. When focused on a Story Mission or Dynamic Op, any important items are automatically added to this slot. This way you and your squad don’t have to make room for critical mission items.
Parachute:
 Whether you have a parachute equipped.
Melee Weapon:
 Allows you to equip melee weapon variants, such as the “Sombra Besada” Combat Knife Blueprint from the Vault Edition’s Signature Weapons Collection.
FOB Firing Range
Available Immediately / Accessed within the FOB
Bring any ranged weapon in your Loadout to the Firing Range, a wooded grove nestled between the Gunsmith and Vendor Stations and test out your build. 
The Firing Range offers multiple walls to crouch or drop into a prone stance, as well as to mount to.
Targets (both circular and bottle-shaped) are available at multiple ranges, from less than 10 meters to over 100. 
Survival Kits Station
Available Immediately / Accessed within the FOB
Survival Kits contains a selection of supplies for use during deployments. Kits focused on medical support, armor, equipment, restocking, or general preparation.
Interact with a Survival Kit to unpack it and add all the contents to your Stash. Unopened kits remain stored within the Survival Kits station menu, where they can stack indefinitely until you are ready to use them.
There are several types of Survival Kits, each with a variety of contents. Examples include: Deployment Kits (essential supplies), Triage Kits (medical), Armor Kits (protective items), Equipment Kits, and SERE Kits for high-risk recoveries.
Acquire Survival Kits as rewards for DMZ Player Leveling, by completing DMZ Orders and Missions, and as Event rewards.
Survival Kit Example: Deployment Kit
Vault Edition Reward: Players with the
 Modern Warfare 4 Vault Edition
will gain access to the Vault Edition Deployment Kit containing:
Weapon Insurance Contracts (x2)
Operator Rescue Beacons (x2)

Gunsmith

The Gunsmith works similarly to Multiplayer: level up specific weapons to unlock attachments along their progression tracks, weapon progression counts across both MP and DMZ. Any compatible attachments you’ve already unlocked through Multiplayer or other means are also available to use in DMZ.
The major difference In DMZ is that Gunsmith weapons are purchased and built using in-game DMZ Cash.

However, to purchase a weapon within the Gunsmith, you must first locate the Weapon Manual for that weapon, and extract with it. Complete this, and you can purchase the weapon as often as you wish to.
