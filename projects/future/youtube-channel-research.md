# Original animated YouTube channel: research brief

Date: 2026-10-29. Scope: research only. No accounts created, no logins, no posts, no spend, no contact. yt-dlp used read-only (metadata and a short auto-caption file, in a temp dir that was then deleted).

Labels: **[V]** = read on the primary page. **[U]** = unverified (search metadata, estimate, or source unreachable). **[E]** = my estimate, not sourced.

## 1. Monetisation rules

- **YPP thresholds [V]** (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/72851): 1,000 subscribers plus either (a) 4,000 qualified public watch hours in the last 12 months, or (b) 10M qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days. Shorts Feed watch hours do not count toward (a). Requires 2-Step Verification, an AdSense account, and no active Community Guidelines strikes. Review usually takes about a month. A rejection can be appealed within 21 days, or re-applied for after 30 days.
- **Early-access tier [U]:** the page only links a video for this. I did not find its thresholds.
- **Shorts revenue share [V]** (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12504220): from 1 Feb 2027, 10M qualified Shorts views in 90 days per month to earn Shorts ad share. Shorts with claimed music over 1 minute are blocked.
- **Inauthentic content [V]** (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392, renamed from "repetitious" on 15 Jul 2025): allowed are "a series following a set of characters across episodes" if each video has a distinct storyline. **Not allowed:** "videos where characters are put in the same situation over and over again with the same outcome", "templated storylines", and AI content "made with generic or unoriginal templates". A repeated mukbang format is exactly this risk unless each episode has its own plot.
- **Policy timeline [V]** (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10008196): newest entries are Aug 2026 (views counted from first frame, which can shift view-based thresholds) and Sept/Oct 2026 (branded content and public-interest content). I found no newer repetitive-content change after Jul 2025.
- **AI disclosure [V]** (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/14328491): disclose only realistic or meaningfully altered AI content. Fully animated, non-realistic content, including AI animation, does not need disclosure. AI music that is the main focus must be disclosed. Cloning your own voice for voice-over does not need disclosure. Disclosure does not reduce monetisation, but YouTube may auto-label animated AI content.
- **Made for kids [V]** (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9528076; FTC guidance at https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/blog/2019/11/youtube-channel-owners-your-content-directed-children): characters, games, songs or stories aimed at children make content "likely made for kids". The creator must set the audience, and YouTube's systems do not do it for you. A disclaimer alone does not settle it. Kids are under 13 in the US. "Animated content that appeals to everyone" is listed as general audience.
- **Made-for-kids effects [V]** (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9527654; https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9208564): no comments, no notification bell, no cards or end screens, no live chat, no Save to playlist, no Miniplayer, no memberships or Super Chat, and **no personalised ads**, which lowers revenue.
- **Kids quality principles [V]** (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10774223): for kids content, low-quality signals include "heavily promotional" (food brands and logos), "unhealthy eating habits", "hard to follow", and "strange use of children's characters". A channel with a strong low-quality focus can be removed from YPP.
- **What this means for a mukbang-style cartoon:** choosing "made for kids" means losing comments, end screens and personalised ads, and the food content is judged against the kids quality rules. Choosing "not made for kids" is only defensible if the characters and humour are genuinely aimed at adults or teens. This is not legal advice. Get counsel before publishing anything child-targeted.

## 2. The format

**Reference: LINE FRIENDS, "SALLY's #MUKBANG" [V via yt-dlp, 2026-10-29]**
- Brand channel: 838K subscribers, 355 uploads (https://www.youtube.com/channel/UC9HtPH9bBaUHueFdb5ygP5Q).
- Series of compilations, each 4.8 to 6.5 minutes. Titles are "Part 1–4" and "Collection 5", with the description saying "more fun to watch in order".
- Part 1: 292s, 26.1M views, uploaded 4 Feb 2023. Part 2: 285s, 16.7M, 5 Feb 2023. Part 3: 324s, 6.2M, 16 Sep 2024. Part 4: 379s, 3.7M, 18 Sep 2024. Collection 5: 389s, 1.7M, 20 Apr 2025.
- Cadence is irregular: batches a few months to a year apart, not weekly. Views fall off sharply for later parts.
- Note: these run over 3 minutes, so they are long-form videos, not Shorts, even though they carry the #shorts tag.
- Auto-captions are only [Music], [Applause] and "foreign" tags, with almost no English words. The format is therefore nearly wordless and driven by music and eating sounds [V for captions; the ASMR reading is my inference]. Auto-captions are noisy, so treat this as indicative.
- Why it may work [E]: short self-contained clips, no language barrier, series binge-watching, cute characters.

## 3. Production options

**Tools and licences**
- **Blender:** GPL, free for any purpose [V] (https://www.blender.org/about/license/). 3D, so the most work.
- **Synfig:** free, GPL v3 [V] (https://www.synfig.org/). 2D vector animation, a good fit for rigged cutout characters.
- **Cavalry:** "Free for Individuals ... completely free to download and use" [V] (https://cavalry.studio/en/). Commercial terms for the free tier are not verified [U].
- **Godot:** MIT, free for any purpose [V] (https://godotengine.org/license/). A game engine, not really needed for a video.
- **Krita:** free and open source, but I did not read the licence page [U].
- **Freesound:** CC0 (no attribution), CC-BY (attribution required), and CC-BY-NC (not for commercial use). Freesound sounds can still be copyright-claimed on YouTube [V] (https://freesound.org/help/faq/).
- **ElevenLabs TTS:** the free tier does not list a commercial licence; Starter does [V] (https://elevenlabs.io/pricing). Prices shown on that page are promotional.
- **AI video:** Runway lists plans "from $12/month" [V] (https://runway.com/pricing); its free tier and commercial terms are not verified [U]. Canva's licence page returned 403 [U].

**Hours per 3–5 minute video for a beginner [E, no source found]**
- 2D rigged cutout (Synfig or Cavalry), reusing a rig: 8–12 hours. First video with new rigs: 20–30 hours.
- Blender 3D: 30–60 hours for the first video, 15–25 hours after that.
- AI-assisted (image generation plus animation, no real lip-sync): 4–8 hours, with the highest character-consistency and "mass-produced" risk.
- Voice: own voice 1–2 hours; TTS about 0.5 hours plus credits. Sound effects: 1–2 hours.

Character consistency is the main production unknown. I have no tested data on how well current AI tools keep one character's design stable across episodes [U].

## 4. Growth math

- I found no non-promotional dataset for time to 1,000 subscribers or 4,000 watch hours [U]. The figures below are arithmetic on the policy thresholds, not measured results.
- **Long-form route:** 4,000 hours = 240,000 minutes. At 5-minute videos and 40% average view duration (my assumption, 2 minutes per view), that is about 120,000 long-form views in 12 months [E].
- **Shorts route:** 10M Shorts views in 90 days is about 111,000 views per day on average [V threshold; arithmetic mine]. The Shorts Feed does not count toward the 4,000 hours.
- **Clip loop:** Shorts are up to 3 minutes [V] (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877). The conversion rate from a Short to long-form is unknown [U]. Made-for-kids status removes end screens and comments, which weakens the loop.
- The share of new channels that reach either threshold is unknown [U]. The brand channel's reach is not comparable to a new channel.

## 5. Risks and unknowns

- **IP and lookalike risk:** designs must not resemble LINE FRIENDS' characters. I did not check their trademarks [U]. Several high-view competitors use third-party IP, which carries copyright and Content ID risk.
- **Kids rules:** see section 1. Food-related kids content is judged on "unhealthy eating" and "heavily promotional" signals.
- **AI and repetition:** a repeated template with AI art risks the inauthentic-content policy, even with disclosure.
- **Burnout:** 5-minute episodes at weekly cadence would mean 8–30 hours per week for the 2D or 3D routes [E].
- **Policy change:** the view-counting change (Aug 2026) and the Shorts threshold from 2027 can shift the targets.

## 6. Verdict and decisions

**Verdict:** The format is cheap to make, but a repeated mukbang template is exactly what YouTube's inauthentic-content rule penalises. It works only if every episode has a distinct storyline, and the kids label is the largest trap. Pursue it only if William accepts a story-driven format, general-audience positioning, and a 2D production route. Otherwise skip it. This is my recommendation, not a conclusion from data.

**Three decisions William must make first:**
1. **Format:** repeated food compilations, or episodic stories where food is one segment?
2. **Production:** 2D cutout (Synfig or Cavalry), Blender 3D, or AI-assisted?
3. **Audience:** general audience (not made for kids), or kids? Kids costs comments, end screens and personalised ads, and is judged on quality rules.

**Smallest $0 validation experiment (time-boxed, no publishing):**
- Time box: 10 hours over 2 weeks.
- Build one original character and two different 60–90 second episodes with different plots, in Synfig or Cavalry. Use only CC0 or CC-BY Freesound effects, with attribution logged. No publishing and no account creation without William's approval.
- Pass: total build time at or under 10 hours; each episode has a different plot (check against the "same situation" rule); at least 3 of 10 people William shows it to ask for the next episode unprompted. (Showing it to people is William's call.)
- Fail: more than 15 hours for the two episodes, the plots repeat, or no one asks for more.
- Before any upload: William decides the audience setting, and counsel reviews it if the content may target children.
