# Animated YouTube channel: creative options (not kids content)

Date 2026-10-29. Research only, nothing created or posted. Labels: **[V]** seen on the public page, **[U]** unverified, **[E]** my estimate.
**Method note:** I had no shell in this run, so yt-dlp was not used. I read public channel pages (subs, video-tab view counts, "x ago") in the browser, logged out. No temp files. "Recent" = last ~10 uploads at 2026-10-29. Per-video views come from the page listing, so rounding is YouTube's.
**Kids rule used for every concept [V]** (https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9528076): the creator sets the audience. Content with characters, stories or games aimed at children is "made for kids", while animation that appeals to everyone is general audience. So "not for kids" depends on theme, humour and marketing, not on being animated. Cute art is the risk; keep character design and themes adult or teen. Made-for-kids loses comments, end screens and personalised ads [V, see research file].

## The concepts

**1. Original animated horror anthology** (distinct fiction story each episode)
- Premise/sample: "The Morgue"-style 8-10 min stories, new cast each time. Sample: night-shift worker finds the building has one more floor than the plan.
- Audience: teens 14+/adults; mature themes. Not made for kids.
- Demand: Animated Horror Flicks, 171K subs, 59 videos, one creator; last 5 uploads 60K-427K [V]. Llama Arts 2.57M subs, 107 videos, newest long videos 276K-376K, cadence slow [V]. DarkAce Animations 96.3K subs, 535 videos, 3.5K-29K per video, last upload ~1y ago [V]: volume without originality plateaus. Many more channels in the same search (MJV 605K, IMR Scary Tales 1.24M, Mort 550K) [V subs only].
- Production: limited animation / 2.5D cutout; mostly static scenes with camera moves. 15-40 h per 8 min episode with agents on script/storyboard/voice [E].
- Monetisation: horror is often limited-ads [U], so RPM likely below explainers [E]. Strong Shorts clip potential (scare beats) [E].
- Risks: "true story" framing for invented tales would be deceptive, so label as fiction. Gore/suicide topics hurt ad-friendliness. Crowded lane. Burnout is moderate.

**2. Animated history / "the entire history of X"**
- Premise/sample: 20-35 min map-and-character essays. Sample: "How a Single Salt Tax Built an Empire."
- Audience: 16-35 general audience; Not made for kids.
- Demand: Animated History, **99.3K subs, only 20 videos**, recent uploads 46K-1.3M (e.g. 35 min Israel history 1.3M, 25 min China 1.1M, 32 min Germany 810K) [V]. SideQuest 677K subs [V subs only]. OverSimplified 9.68M subs, 33 videos, 20M per part, but one video every year or so and heavy production [V]. This is the best small-channel evidence I found.
- Production: map animation plus cutout characters; 30-60 h per 25 min video [E]. Research and fact-checking dominate, which suits an agent team.
- Monetisation: long watch time helps the 4,000 hours (a 25 min video at 40% retention is 10 min per view [E]). War/genocide topics may get limited ads [U].
- Risks: factual errors, political sensitivity, image/music copyright, and "AI-slop history" reputation. Needs a human fact-check pass.

**3. Psychology / mental-health / money explainers with a recurring original character host**
- Premise/sample: 5-7 min "why do you ..." videos. Sample: "Why you can't stop doomscrolling".
- Audience: 16-30; not made for kids.
- Demand: Psych2Go 13.2M subs, 4K videos; recent 5-7 min videos 26K-478K, median ~60K [V]. A big channel's median is modest. Finance niche: search shows many brand-new "2D animated finance explainer" channels with 1-31 subs [V], and Penny Explains Finance 2.67K [V]: a saturation signal. Kurzgesagt 25.8M subs, 4.8M-22M per video, but a large studio [V].
- Production: easiest: illustration, light motion. 6-15 h per 6 min video [E].
- Monetisation: finance has high RPM [E, unsourced]; mental-health topics can be sensitive. Psych2Go has members-only videos [V].
- Risks: generic template = inauthentic-content policy; medical/financial claims need sourcing; the lane is crowded.

**4. Fiction-lore / worldbuilding series (SCP-style, or our own universe)**
- Premise/sample: lore dossiers for a shared fictional universe. Sample: "File 031: The Door in Room 9".
- Audience: teens/young adults. Not made for kids.
- Demand: SCP Explained 2.49M subs, 1.8K videos; recent 14 min videos 37K-50K, 2h compilations 27K-150K [V]. SCP content is shared under CC-BY-SA [U, licence not checked]; our own universe avoids that and lets Shorts/TikTok clips point at it.
- Production: limited animation, heavy use of reused assets; 8-20 h per 14 min [E].
- Monetisation: similar to horror [E]. Compilations of existing episodes add watch hours but look "repetitive" if overdone.
- Risks: slow start for an unknown universe, and SCP licence conditions if we borrow.

**5. Original adult/teen comedy series (sitcom or sketch)**
- Premise/sample: 3-8 min episodes with 3-4 recurring characters. Sample: roommates discover the apartment's ghost is a landlord.
- Audience: 16-30. Not for kids if the humour is adult/satirical.
- Demand: huge ceiling but a brutal start. GLITCH 23.5M subs, recent episodes 13M-90M [V] (large funded team). hotdiggedydemon 2.21M subs, but top views date 3-7 years ago [V]. Floor: Tavern Toons (313 subs) 25-3.6K, mostly <300 [V]. SheddiAnime 1.57M subs: 19M-view compilation built on horror-film characters, and one video titled "Demonetized (Misleading Family Content claim)" [V]. Third-party IP plus kid-adjacent style has real cost.
- Production: highest of the story concepts: full character acting, lip sync. 40-100+ h per 5 min [E].
- Monetisation: good if mainstream-safe [E]; no hard evidence.
- Risks: needs genuinely funny writing; highest burnout; IP temptations.

**6. Animated true stories / storytime** (submitted or fictionalised, clearly labelled)
- Premise: relationship/school/work stories told by a voice over a simple character. Sample: "I got locked out during my own surprise party."
- Audience: teen/young adult. Many sit near the kids line (school-age narrators).
- Demand: Storybooth 4.56M subs; only one recent video (60K), earlier hits 230K-770K [V]. MSA 27.7M subs [V subs only]. Counter-evidence: YASS 465K subs, 1.1K videos, 90 min uploads drawing ~600-1.1K views each [V]: mass-produced story farms collapse.
- Production: cheap (simple cutout, 6-12 h per 5 min [E]).
- Risks: fake-"true" claims are deceptive; mass production triggers the inauthentic-content rule; teen narrators push toward made-for-kids scrutiny.

**7. Cozy slice-of-life (adult comfort, Ghibli-ish)**
- Premise/sample: wordless 10-20 min episodes. Sample: a baker's rainy morning.
- Audience: adults who want calm; Not made for kids if no children's characters.
- Demand: weak in what I found. Animotion Dreamverse (26.2K subs, AI-generated art disclosed in its bio): 135-1.9K per video [V]. Dozens of 4-358 sub cozy channels [V]. No evidence of a successful small original cozy channel.
- Risks: "Ghibli-style" labels invite IP complaints; AI cozy is crowded.

**8. Animated reaction / podcast with character avatars**
- Not enough evidence: my searches returned only tiny channels (3-602 subs) and no animated-avatar channel with measurable views [V]. Treat as unproven [U]. Low production cost [E], but needs a real personality writing the talk, otherwise it fails the "original voice" test.

## Ranked shortlist (demand x feasibility with an agent-assisted team x low risk)
1. **Original horror anthology** (concept 1). Proven demand with a one-person small channel (171K subs), easy limited animation, strong Shorts cutdowns, and distinct stories avoid the repetition rule [V policy in research file]. Main cost is lower ad-friendliness [U].
2. **Animated history essays** (concept 2). Best small-channel numbers (99K subs, 20 videos, up to 1.3M), and an agent team does the heaviest work (research, scripting). Needs human fact-checking and long runtimes.
3. **Psychology/explainer with an original host** (concept 3). Cheapest to make, but crowded and generic. Rank 3 only if the host is distinctive.

## Prototype first
One 6-8 min original horror story (concept 1), produced with limited animation, plus 3 Shorts cut from it. Time-box ~15 h, as an experiment only, no publishing without William's go. Measure: hours spent, whether the story holds up when watched cold, and whether the 3 clips read as complete. Run the same script test for one history outline (2 h) as comparison.
